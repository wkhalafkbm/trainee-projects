# Performance Review Process

**Clearance Level:** HR-Only  
**Last Updated:** January 2025  
**Owner:** Human Resources

---

## Annual Review Cycle and Timeline

The company runs one formal performance review cycle per year, aligned to the fiscal year (January–December).

| Period | Activity |
|---|---|
| November | Employees complete self-assessments via the HR portal |
| Early December | Line managers submit draft ratings |
| Mid December | Calibration sessions across departments |
| Late December | Ratings finalised and communicated to employees |
| January (following year) | Salary adjustments take effect |

Mid-year check-ins are informal and are not recorded as formal ratings. They serve as a course-correction opportunity for employees who are off-track and should not be used as evidence in a year-end review unless the employee has been placed on a formal Performance Improvement Plan.

---

## Rating Scale

Performance is rated on a five-point scale:

| Rating | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Exceptional | Consistently exceeds all expectations; demonstrable impact beyond role scope |
| 4 | Exceeds Expectations | Regularly delivers above what is required for the role |
| 3 | Meets Expectations | Delivers what is required reliably; this is the expected rating for a fully performing employee |
| 2 | Partially Meets Expectations | Inconsistent performance; improvement required in key areas |
| 1 | Does Not Meet Expectations | Significant performance gaps; formal action required |

A rating of 3 (Meets Expectations) should be the modal outcome for a healthy team. Calibration sessions exist specifically to prevent rating inflation, where managers systematically rate their teams higher than the cross-company distribution supports.

---

## Calibration Process

Calibration sessions are held at the departmental level and attended by all line managers within a function and a senior HR Business Partner.

The purpose of calibration is to:
- Ensure that the same behaviours are rated consistently across different managers
- Identify employees who have been over- or under-rated relative to their peers
- Ensure the distribution of ratings is reasonable across the function

During calibration, managers are expected to be able to provide specific, behavioural evidence for any rating of 4 or 5, and for any rating of 1 or 2. A rating without supporting evidence may be adjusted. What is said in calibration is confidential and may not be shared with employees.

---

## Link Between Performance Ratings and Salary Reviews

Salary review outcomes are linked to performance ratings according to the following guidelines. These are guidelines, not guarantees — final decisions are subject to budget availability and management discretion.

| Rating | Typical Salary Review Outcome |
|---|---|
| 5 (Exceptional) | Merit increase of 8–12% |
| 4 (Exceeds Expectations) | Merit increase of 4–7% |
| 3 (Meets Expectations) | Merit increase of 1–3% (cost of living adjustment) |
| 2 (Partially Meets Expectations) | No increase; may receive a development-focused review in Q2 |
| 1 (Does Not Meet Expectations) | No increase; formal performance action |

Employees are not permitted to discuss salary review amounts with colleagues. HR will communicate individual outcomes directly.

## Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs)

A Performance Improvement Plan is a formal document issued to employees rated 1 (Does Not Meet Expectations), or in cases of sustained 2-level performance across two consecutive review cycles.

A PIP must:
- Be initiated by the line manager and co-signed by the HR Business Partner
- Define specific, measurable improvement targets over a 90-day period
- Include a schedule of check-in meetings (at minimum, bi-weekly)
- Clearly state the consequence of not meeting the targets within the plan period

A PIP is not a precursor to termination — it is a structured support mechanism. However, failure to meet the targets defined in a PIP within the agreed timeframe may result in a change of role or separation, following the procedures set out in the Employment Policy.

All PIPs are stored in the employee's confidential HR file and are not visible to colleagues or other managers.

---

## Raising a Dispute About a Performance Rating

Employees who believe their performance rating is inaccurate or unfair may raise a formal dispute within 14 calendar days of receiving their year-end rating.

**Process:**

1. Submit a written dispute to the HR Business Partner assigned to your department
2. HR will acknowledge receipt within 3 business days
3. A review meeting will be convened with the employee, line manager, and HR Business Partner within 10 business days
4. If the dispute is upheld, the rating may be adjusted. If not upheld, the original rating stands and a written explanation is provided to the employee.

Raising a dispute will not result in any negative consequence for the employee. All disputes are treated confidentially.
