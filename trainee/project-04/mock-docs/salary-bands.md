# Salary Banding and Compensation Structure

**Clearance Level:** Management-Only  
**Last Updated:** January 2025  
**Owner:** Total Rewards, Human Resources

---

## Job Grade Structure

The company uses a seven-level job grade structure. All roles are mapped to a grade. Compensation ranges are expressed in Kuwaiti Dinar (KWD) per month (gross).

| Grade | Level Title | Monthly Salary Range (KWD) | Typical Roles |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Associate | 400 – 650 | Junior Analyst, Administrative Assistant, Support Coordinator |
| G2 | Analyst | 650 – 950 | Analyst, Associate Specialist, Coordinator |
| G3 | Senior Analyst | 950 – 1,400 | Senior Analyst, Specialist, Team Lead (small team) |
| G4 | Manager | 1,400 – 2,100 | Manager, Senior Specialist, Project Lead |
| G5 | Senior Manager | 2,100 – 3,000 | Senior Manager, Principal, Department Head (small dept) |
| G6 | Director | 3,000 – 4,500 | Director, Head of Function |
| G7 | Executive | 4,500 – 7,000+ | VP, C-Suite |

Salaries are set within the band at hire based on candidate experience, internal equity, and budget. Employees may be informed of their grade but not of the full band range. Sharing band range information with employees outside of a compensation discussion initiated by HR is prohibited.

---

## How Promotions Affect Compensation

A promotion moves an employee from one grade to the next. Promotion decisions are made during the annual review cycle (or, in exceptional cases, off-cycle with VP approval).

**Compensation adjustment on promotion:**

- The employee's new salary is set at or above the minimum of the new grade
- The typical uplift on promotion is 10–20% of current base salary, subject to:
  - The new salary falling within the new grade's band
  - Available budget within the receiving department
- If the employee's current salary already falls within the new grade's band (rare, but possible after sustained merit increases), the uplift may be smaller

Promotions are communicated to employees by the line manager, with HR present. The new grade and salary are confirmed in writing within 5 business days of the communication.

## Merit Increase Budget Allocation

The annual merit increase budget is set by the Finance function as a percentage of total payroll cost. This percentage varies year to year based on company performance and market conditions.

**Distribution of the merit budget:**

- Department heads receive a budget envelope expressed as a percentage of their department's total payroll
- Within that envelope, they allocate increases to individuals based on performance ratings (see Performance Review Process for rating-to-increase guidelines)
- Department heads may not exceed their envelope in aggregate. They may redistribute within it (e.g. give a higher increase to a top performer by giving nothing to a lower performer), subject to the constraints in the Performance Review Policy

Unused merit budget does not roll forward to the next cycle. Requests to exceed the envelope require CFO approval and are rarely granted outside of exceptional circumstances (e.g. critical retention risk).

## Market Benchmarking

Salary bands are reviewed annually against external market data from two sources:

1. **Mercer Total Remuneration Survey (Kuwait)** — primary benchmark for most grades
2. **Hay Group Gulf Compensation Survey** — used as a secondary reference for G5 and above

The benchmarking exercise is conducted by the Total Rewards team in Q3 each year. Results are reviewed by the CHRO and presented to the Executive Committee.

**Benchmarking targets:**

| Grade | Market Position Target |
|---|---|
| G1 – G3 | Median (50th percentile) |
| G4 – G5 | 60th percentile |
| G6 – G7 | 65th–75th percentile |

If a grade's band falls below the target percentile, an out-of-cycle band adjustment may be made. This does not automatically increase the salary of employees already within the old band — it widens the range and affects new hires and promotees first.

Employees whose current salary falls below the new band minimum following a band adjustment are brought up to the minimum in the next payroll cycle without waiting for the annual review.
