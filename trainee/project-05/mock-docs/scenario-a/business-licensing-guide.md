# Business Licensing in Kuwait: A Complete Guide
## Ministry of Commerce and Industry — Licensing Directorate

**Document Reference:** MOC-LIC-001  
**Version:** 3.2  
**Last Updated:** March 2025  
**Issuing Authority:** Licensing and Registration Directorate, Ministry of Commerce and Industry (MoCI)

---

## Introduction

Operating a business in Kuwait requires obtaining the appropriate commercial license issued by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry (MoCI). This guide outlines the main categories of business licenses available, their eligibility requirements, required documentation, applicable fees, and expected processing timelines.

All businesses operating within Kuwait must maintain a valid license at all times. Operating without a valid license is a violation of Commercial Companies Law No. 1 of 2016 and its amendments, and is subject to penalties including fines and forced closure.

This guide is intended as a general reference. For activity-specific guidance or complex ownership structures, applicants are advised to consult the relevant licensing desk at MoCI or an accredited legal advisor.

---

## Section 1: License Categories Overview

Kuwait's business licensing framework recognizes four primary license categories:

| License Type | Primary Use | Issuing Body |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial License | Trading and import/export | MoCI — Commercial Registry |
| Industrial License | Manufacturing and production | MoCI — Industrial Affairs |
| Professional License | Services and consultancy | MoCI — Professional Registry |
| Crafts License | Small trades and artisanal work | MoCI — Small Business Unit |

Each category carries distinct requirements, fee structures, and renewal obligations. Choosing the wrong license category is one of the most common errors made by applicants and typically requires a full re-application.

## Section 2: Commercial License (Trading)

### 2.1 Overview

A Commercial License (sometimes called a Trading License) authorizes the holder to engage in the buying and selling of goods, import and export activities, and general trading operations. It is the most commonly issued license type in Kuwait and covers a wide range of activities from retail shops to wholesale distribution and e-commerce operations.

### 2.2 Who It Is For

- Retailers and wholesalers
- Import/export companies
- Distributors and agents for foreign brands
- E-commerce businesses selling physical goods
- General trading companies

### 2.3 Eligibility Requirements

- Applicant must be at least 21 years of age
- Kuwaiti nationals may apply as a sole proprietor or through a company structure
- Non-Kuwaiti nationals may not hold a Commercial License as a sole proprietor; they must operate through a company in which a Kuwaiti national holds at least 51% ownership (see Section 7 on Foreign Ownership)
- The proposed business activity must not appear on the Restricted Activities list (see Section 8)
- Applicant must not have any outstanding judgment debts to the Kuwaiti government
- For companies: the company must be registered in the Commercial Registry before a license application is submitted

### 2.4 Required Documents

1. Completed MoCI application form (Form LIC-C-01), signed and stamped
2. Copy of Civil ID (for Kuwaiti applicants) or Residency Permit / Iqama (for expats acting through a company)
3. Original Commercial Registry extract (for companies), issued within the last 90 days
4. Memorandum and Articles of Association (for companies), notarized
5. Proof of registered business address — one of the following:
   - Original lease agreement (minimum 1-year term, notarized or certified by the relevant municipality)
   - Title deed if premises are owned
6. Passport copy of all shareholders (if applicable)
7. No-objection letter from the landlord (if the premises are shared with another business)
8. Municipality clearance certificate (Baladiya)
9. For import/export activities: import/export registration form (Form CUST-02)
10. Payment receipt for licensing fee

### 2.5 Fees

| Fee Item | Amount (KWD) |
|---|---|
| Application processing fee | 75 |
| License issuance fee (annual) | 250 |
| Commercial Registry registration | 100 |
| Import/export endorsement (if applicable) | 150 |
| Notarization and attestation (estimated) | 30–80 |
| **Estimated total (first year)** | **505–605** |

Fees are subject to change without notice. Always confirm current fee schedules at the MoCI fee counter (Window 14, Ground Floor, Ministries Complex) or via the Sahel government portal.

### 2.6 Processing Time

Standard processing time for a Commercial License is **10 to 15 working days** from the date of submission of a complete application. Applications with missing documents will be returned and the processing clock will restart upon resubmission.

Expedited processing (priority queue) is available for an additional fee of KWD 100 and reduces processing time to **5 working days**.

> **Note:** Processing times stated here reflect MoCI's standard service levels. Actual timelines may vary during peak periods (beginning and end of the fiscal year) or if the application is referred to another ministry for activity approval. The FAQ document (Section 12 of the FAQ) states a general guideline of 7–10 days, which reflects best-case conditions with a fully complete application.

## Section 3: Industrial License (Manufacturing)

### 3.1 Overview

An Industrial License is required for any business engaged in manufacturing, processing, assembling, or transforming raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods within Kuwait. Industrial licenses are subject to additional oversight from the Public Authority for Industry (PAI) in addition to MoCI requirements.

### 3.2 Who It Is For

- Food and beverage manufacturers
- Garment and textile factories
- Metal fabrication and engineering workshops
- Chemical and plastics manufacturers
- Packaging companies
- Printing and publishing factories

### 3.3 Eligibility Requirements

- Applicant (individual or company) must comply with all Commercial License eligibility requirements (see 2.3)
- The proposed industrial facility must be located in a designated industrial zone approved by Kuwait Municipality (Farwaniya, Shuwaikh Industrial Area, Mina Abdullah Industrial Zone, or other PAI-approved areas)
- An Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) must be submitted for activities involving chemicals, heavy machinery, or waste-generating processes
- Minimum capital requirement: KWD 10,000 for sole proprietors; KWD 25,000 for companies
- The facility must meet Kuwait Fire Service Directorate safety standards prior to license issuance
- Foreign nationals may participate in industrial companies but Kuwait national ownership must be at least 51% unless operating under a special investment zone agreement approved by the Kuwait Direct Investment Promotion Authority (KDIPA)

### 3.4 Required Documents

1. All documents listed in Section 2.4 (Commercial License documents)
2. Completed PAI application form (Form PAI-01)
3. Facility floor plan and layout drawings (approved by Kuwait Municipality)
4. Environmental compliance declaration or full EIA report (depending on activity category)
5. Fire safety inspection certificate from Kuwait Fire Service Directorate
6. List of machinery and equipment (including make, model, capacity, country of origin)
7. Proof of industrial zone tenancy (lease from Kuwait Municipality or relevant authority)
8. Health and safety plan signed by a qualified safety officer
9. For food manufacturing: approval from the Ministry of Health (Food Safety Department)

### 3.5 Fees

| Fee Item | Amount (KWD) |
|---|---|
| MoCI application fee | 75 |
| Industrial license issuance fee | 400 |
| PAI registration fee | 200 |
| Environmental compliance fee (if EIA required) | 150–500 |
| Fire safety inspection fee | 80 |
| **Estimated total (first year)** | **905–1,255** |

### 3.6 Processing Time

Industrial license applications typically take **20 to 30 working days** due to the multi-agency review process. Applications requiring an Environmental Impact Assessment may take up to **60 working days**.

## Section 4: Professional License (Services and Consultancies)

### 4.1 Overview

A Professional License authorizes the holder to provide services based on professional expertise, skills, or qualifications. Unlike commercial licenses, professional licenses are typically tied to the individual professional's qualifications, not just the legal entity. Examples include law firms, engineering consultancies, medical clinics, accounting offices, IT companies, and management consulting firms.

### 4.2 Who It Is For

- Legal practitioners and law firms
- Engineering and architecture firms
- Medical and dental practices
- Accounting and auditing firms
- IT and technology consulting companies
- Management and business consultancies
- Marketing and advertising agencies
- Translation and interpretation offices

### 4.3 Eligibility Requirements

- At least one partner or director must hold a recognized professional qualification relevant to the licensed activity (e.g., a law firm must have a licensed attorney; an engineering firm must have a licensed engineer)
- Professional qualifications must be attested by the relevant Kuwait professional body (e.g., Kuwait Bar Association for lawyers, Kuwait Society of Engineers for engineers)
- For regulated professions (medicine, law, pharmacy, engineering): registration with the relevant syndicate or association is mandatory before a Professional License can be issued
- Foreign professionals may be named as technical directors but the license must be held in the name of a Kuwaiti national or a company with at least 51% Kuwaiti ownership, unless the activity is listed under KDIPA's 100% foreign ownership schedule (see Section 7)
- Minimum capital requirements do not apply to professional sole proprietorships but do apply to professional companies (minimum KWD 5,000)

### 4.4 Required Documents

1. Completed MoCI application form (Form LIC-P-01)
2. Copy of Civil ID or Residency Permit for all named principals
3. Certificates of professional qualification for the named professional director(s), attested by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the relevant Kuwaiti professional body
4. Certificate of membership/registration from the relevant Kuwaiti professional association
5. No-objection certificate from the relevant ministry (e.g., Ministry of Health for medical activities; Ministry of Justice for legal activities)
6. Proof of office address (lease or title deed)
7. Municipality clearance certificate
8. For companies: Commercial Registry extract and Memorandum of Association

### 4.5 Fees

| Fee Item | Amount (KWD) |
|---|---|
| Application processing fee | 75 |
| Professional license issuance fee | 175 |
| Professional body registration (varies) | 50–200 |
| **Estimated total (first year)** | **300–450** |

Professional licenses are generally less expensive than commercial or industrial licenses, reflecting the lower regulatory burden on service-based activities.

### 4.6 Processing Time

Professional license applications are processed in **7 to 12 working days**, provided all professional qualifications have been pre-verified by the relevant bodies. Verification of foreign qualifications can add 2 to 4 additional weeks.

## Section 5: Crafts License (Small Trades)

### 5.1 Overview

A Crafts License is designed for small-scale, skill-based operations typically run by individual artisans or small workshops. This license covers activities such as tailoring, carpentry, auto repair, electrical installation, plumbing, hairdressing, and similar trades. The Crafts License is simpler and less expensive than a full Commercial License and is administered through MoCI's Small Business Unit.

### 5.2 Who It Is For

- Independent tailors and seamstresses
- Carpenters and furniture makers
- Auto mechanics and car body repair shops
- Electricians, plumbers, and HVAC technicians
- Hairdressers and barbers
- Cobblers and leather repair craftspeople
- Welders and metal fabricators (small scale)
- Bakers and pastry makers (home-based or small commercial kitchens)

### 5.3 Eligibility Requirements

- Applicant must be at least 18 years of age (lower than other license types)
- The activity must appear on MoCI's approved Crafts Activities list (updated annually)
- Expats may apply for a Crafts License under their own name, but must hold a valid residency permit with "commercial" or "free" (hur) transfer category
- Kuwaiti nationals may apply as sole proprietors with no minimum capital requirement
- Expat applicants must provide a Kuwaiti national sponsor (kafeel) unless the activity is on the MoCI exemption list
- Maximum number of employees: 10 (exceeding this threshold requires upgrading to a Commercial License)

### 5.4 Required Documents

1. Completed MoCI application form (Form LIC-CR-01)
2. Copy of Civil ID (Kuwaiti) or Residency Permit and Passport (expat)
3. Proof of trade skill — one of: vocational training certificate, apprenticeship record, or letter from a recognized trade association
4. Proof of business premises (lease or municipality-approved workshop certificate)
5. Municipality clearance certificate
6. For expats: copy of sponsor's (kafeel's) Civil ID and a notarized no-objection letter from the sponsor

### 5.5 Fees

| Fee Item | Amount (KWD) |
|---|---|
| Application processing fee | 25 |
| Crafts license issuance fee | 50 |
| **Estimated total (first year)** | **75** |

The Crafts License is the most affordable license category, reflecting the government's policy of supporting small-scale entrepreneurship and traditional trades.

### 5.6 Processing Time

Crafts license applications are typically processed within **5 to 7 working days**. This is the fastest turnaround among all license categories.

## Section 6: General Requirements Applicable to All License Types

Regardless of the license type, all applicants must:

- Ensure the business name does not infringe on existing trademarks registered with the Patents and Trademarks Department
- Confirm the intended business address is zoned for the proposed activity (residential addresses are generally not permitted for Commercial or Industrial activities)
- Obtain a Municipality Clearance Certificate (Baladiya) confirming the premises meet building and health codes
- Renew the license annually (see separate Renewal and Amendments guide)
- Display the license prominently at the principal place of business
- Notify MoCI within 30 days of any change to the information recorded on the license

## Section 7: Foreign Ownership Rules

### 7.1 General Rule

Kuwait's general rule under Commercial Companies Law No. 1 of 2016 requires that companies operating in Kuwait have a Kuwaiti national partner holding at least **51% of the share capital**. This applies to Commercial, Industrial, and most Professional License categories.

The Kuwaiti partner (often called the local partner or sponsor) plays a legal role in the company structure and must be named in the Commercial Registry. Silent partnership arrangements (where the Kuwaiti partner holds shares on paper only) are technically prohibited, though enforcement has varied historically.

### 7.2 Exceptions: 100% Foreign Ownership

Full foreign ownership (100%) is permitted in the following circumstances:

1. **Kuwait Direct Investment Promotion Authority (KDIPA) licensed companies:** Companies that obtain a special license from KDIPA under Law No. 116 of 2013 may operate with 100% foreign ownership in specific sectors. KDIPA-approved sectors include information technology, logistics, healthcare, education, and certain manufacturing activities. KDIPA applications are assessed on a case-by-case basis and must demonstrate economic benefit to Kuwait.

2. **Free Zones:** Companies registered and operating within Kuwait's special economic zones (including Abdali Free Zone and Shuwaikh Free Zone — note: free zone development is ongoing and operational status should be confirmed with the relevant authority) may operate with 100% foreign ownership within the zone.

3. **GCC Nationals:** Citizens of other GCC member states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Bahrain, Qatar, Oman) are generally treated on par with Kuwaiti nationals for most licensing purposes under GCC economic integration agreements, and may hold up to 100% ownership in certain activities.

### 7.3 Professional License Foreign Ownership

For professional activities, foreign professionals may act as technical directors and provide services, but the license entity must comply with the 51% Kuwaiti ownership rule unless a specific exemption is granted by the relevant professional syndicate and MoCI.

### 7.4 Consequences of Non-Compliance

Using a Kuwaiti national as a nominee shareholder (a "sleeping partner" who receives a fee for holding shares but has no real involvement) is prohibited under Ministerial Resolution No. 73 of 2019. Violations may result in license cancellation, financial penalties, and in serious cases, criminal referral.

## Section 8: Restricted Activities Requiring Additional Approvals

Certain business activities require approvals from ministries or regulatory bodies beyond MoCI before a license can be issued. Submitting an application for a restricted activity without the required pre-approval will result in automatic rejection.

### 8.1 Health and Medical Activities

All activities involving the provision of medical, dental, pharmaceutical, or health services require prior approval from the **Ministry of Health (MoH)**. This includes:

- Medical clinics and specialist centers
- Pharmacies and pharmaceutical distribution
- Optical centers
- Physiotherapy and rehabilitation centers
- Veterinary clinics

### 8.2 Financial Services Activities

Activities involving banking, money exchange, insurance, investment management, and financial advisory services require approval from the **Central Bank of Kuwait (CBK)** or the **Capital Markets Authority (CMA)** depending on the nature of the activity.

### 8.3 Educational Activities

Private schools, training institutes, and educational centers require approval from the **Ministry of Education** or the **Private Universities Council** (for higher education institutions).

### 8.4 Legal Services

Law firms and legal advisory practices require registration with the **Ministry of Justice** and membership in the **Kuwait Bar Association**.

### 8.5 Food and Beverage

Restaurants, cafes, catering companies, and food manufacturing require approvals from **Kuwait Municipality** (health and safety inspection) and, for food production, the **Ministry of Health Food Safety Department**.

### 8.6 Security and Surveillance

Private security companies and surveillance equipment installation firms require approval from the **Ministry of Interior**.

### 8.7 Media and Publishing

News outlets, publishing houses, advertising agencies producing broadcast content, and social media commercial accounts require registration with the **Ministry of Information**.

> Applicants should consult MoCI's Restricted Activities Reference List (available at the Licensing Directorate reception or on the Sahel portal) before preparing an application to identify any additional approvals needed.
