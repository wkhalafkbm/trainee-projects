## Section 1: General Eligibility Requirements

All applicants for any Al-Baraka Islamic Bank retail financing product must meet the following baseline requirements:

- **Age:** Minimum 21 years at the time of application. Maximum age at the end of the financing term: 60 years for expat applicants; 65 years for Kuwaiti nationals. (Example: a 55-year-old Kuwaiti national may apply for a maximum 10-year Ijara term; a 55-year-old expat may apply for a maximum 5-year term.)
- **Residency:** Must be a legal resident of Kuwait with a valid Civil ID or Residency Permit (Iqama). The residency permit must remain valid for at least 6 months beyond the expected application processing date.
- **Bank account:** Must hold or be willing to open an active current or savings account with Al-Baraka Islamic Bank. Salary must be transferred to this account for the duration of the financing (salary transfer is a standard condition of retail financing).
- **Credit history:** Must not have any active defaults, court judgments, or CBK-listed bad debts at the time of application. A credit check is conducted through the Credit Information Network (CINET) as part of all applications.
- **Employment:** Must be in stable, documented employment (government, semi-government, or established private sector company). Self-employed applicants are eligible under specific conditions (see Section 2.3).

## Section 2: Minimum Salary Requirements

### 2.1 Kuwaiti Nationals

| Product | Minimum Monthly Salary (KWD) |
|---|---|
| Murabaha (consumer goods) | 350 |
| Tawarruq (personal finance) | 400 |
| Ijara (property — residential) | 600 |
| Ijara (property — commercial) | 800 |

Kuwaiti nationals employed in the government sector or quasi-government entities (including Kuwait Oil Company, Kuwait Airways, and other state-owned enterprises) are assessed favorably due to the perceived job security of government employment.

### 2.2 Expat Applicants

| Product | Minimum Monthly Salary (KWD) |
|---|---|
| Murabaha (consumer goods) | 500 |
| Tawarruq (personal finance) | 550 |
| Ijara (property) | 800 |

Expat applicants face higher minimum salary thresholds because their residency status is tied to their employment, which introduces an additional risk factor — if employment ends, residency may also end, affecting the customer's ability to service the financing. The bank does not offer Ijara for commercial property to expat applicants unless the applicant holds a valid Commercial License and has been operating the business for at least 3 years.

### 2.3 Self-Employed Applicants

Self-employed applicants (business owners, freelancers, and licensed professionals) are eligible for Murabaha and Tawarruq products subject to the following additional conditions:

- Valid Commercial or Professional License issued by MoCI, with at least 2 years of continuous operation
- Audited financial statements for the most recent 2 fiscal years, certified by a licensed auditor
- 6 months of business bank statements showing consistent income
- Minimum declared net income: KWD 600/month for Kuwaiti nationals; KWD 750/month for expats

Self-employed applicants are not eligible for Ijara residential financing unless they also receive a documented fixed salary from their company (some business owners pay themselves a formal salary — this can be used if properly documented).

## Section 3: Maximum Financing Amounts

### 3.1 Murabaha (Consumer Goods)

Maximum financing amounts for Murabaha are determined by a combination of the customer's salary, the value of the specific item being purchased, and the applicable CBK consumer finance guidelines.

| Customer Profile | Maximum Murabaha Amount (KWD) |
|---|---|
| Kuwaiti national, government employed | 20,000 |
| Kuwaiti national, private sector | 15,000 |
| Expat, established private sector (salary > KWD 800) | 10,000 |
| Expat, standard private sector | 7,000 |

The financing amount may not exceed **100% of the item's purchase price** as evidenced by the supplier invoice. The bank does not provide Murabaha financing for amounts exceeding the documented cost of the item.

### 3.2 Tawarruq (Personal Finance)

| Customer Profile | Maximum Tawarruq Amount (KWD) |
|---|---|
| Kuwaiti national, government employed | 15,000 |
| Kuwaiti national, private sector | 10,000 |
| Expat, established private sector (salary > KWD 800) | 8,000 |
| Expat, standard private sector | 5,000 |

Under CBK regulations, the total monthly installment for all personal finance products (across all banks) must not exceed **40% of the applicant's net monthly salary**. The bank will calculate your current debt-to-income ratio as part of the application process. If existing commitments already approach this limit, the financing amount offered may be lower than the maximum shown above.

### 3.3 Ijara (Property Financing)

| Property Type | Maximum Financing (KWD) | Maximum Percentage of Property Value |
|---|---|---|
| Residential — Kuwaiti national | 500,000 | 80% |
| Residential — Expat | 250,000 | 70% |
| Commercial — Kuwaiti national | 750,000 | 75% |

The remaining percentage must be provided as a down payment by the customer before the bank purchases the property. The bank does not finance the down payment through a separate product — the customer must demonstrate that the down payment funds are available before the application is approved.

Property valuations are conducted by an independent valuer approved by Al-Baraka Islamic Bank. The bank finances against the lower of the agreed purchase price or the independent valuation.

## Section 5: Application Process

### Step 1: Initial Inquiry and Pre-Qualification

Before submitting a full application, you can visit any Al-Baraka Islamic Bank branch or call our customer service line (1800-456) to discuss your financing needs. Our representatives can provide an informal pre-qualification assessment based on your salary, the product you are interested in, and the amount you require.

This pre-qualification is not a binding commitment — it is a preliminary indication to help you decide whether to proceed with a full application.

### Step 2: Submit Application and Documents

Collect all required documents (see Section 4) and submit them at any branch together with the completed application form. Applications can also be started online via the Al-Baraka mobile app (document upload required), but final submission must be completed at a branch for identity verification.

Ensure all documents are original or certified copies. Photocopies that are not certified will delay processing.

### Step 3: Credit Assessment

Our credit team reviews your application against CBK guidelines, the bank's internal credit policy, and your CINET credit history. For Ijara applications, the property valuation is ordered at this stage (if not already submitted).

The credit team may contact you for additional information or clarification. Respond promptly to avoid delays.

### Step 4: Sharia Review (Ijara and Complex Murabaha)

For Ijara transactions and Murabaha transactions above KWD 10,000, the Sharia compliance team conducts a transaction-level review to confirm the specific structure meets Sharia requirements. Standard consumer Murabaha transactions below KWD 10,000 follow pre-approved contract templates and do not require individual Sharia review.

### Step 5: Approval and Offer Letter

If your application is approved, you will receive a formal Offer Letter (also called a Term Sheet) detailing:

- The approved product type and amount
- The bank's profit margin and total cost
- Monthly installment amount
- Financing term
- Any conditions attached to the approval (e.g., mandatory insurance, additional documentation)

Review the Offer Letter carefully. You have **10 working days** to accept the offer in writing. If you do not respond within this period, the offer lapses and a new application must be submitted.

### Step 6: Contract Signing

Upon accepting the Offer Letter, you will be invited to the branch to sign the financing contracts. For Ijara transactions, the property purchase and lease agreements are signed at this stage. For Murabaha transactions, the purchase and resale contracts are signed.

You will be asked to sign multiple documents — please read each one before signing. Ask our representative to explain any clause you do not understand.

### Step 7: Disbursement

- **Murabaha:** The bank contacts the supplier directly and arranges payment. The goods are delivered to you or collected by you depending on the arrangement with the supplier.
- **Tawarruq:** Funds are credited to your Al-Baraka account, typically within 1 to 2 working days of contract signing.
- **Ijara:** The bank transfers the purchase price to the property seller. This is typically coordinated with the Real Estate Registration Department to ensure the title is simultaneously transferred to the bank.

## Section 6: Processing Timelines

| Product | Typical Processing Time |
|---|---|
| Murabaha (standard, < KWD 10,000) | 3–5 working days |
| Murabaha (above KWD 10,000) | 5–8 working days |
| Tawarruq | 3–5 working days |
| Ijara (residential) | 10–15 working days (excluding property valuation time) |
| Ijara (commercial) | 15–20 working days |

Processing timelines assume complete and accurate documentation. Applications with missing or incorrect documents will be paused pending resolution, and the above timelines will not apply.

Property valuations for Ijara typically take 5 to 7 additional working days and are conducted independently of the credit review. In practice, most Ijara applications are fully completed within 3 to 4 weeks of initial submission.

## Section 7: Insurance Requirements

For Ijara financing, the financed asset must be insured for its full replacement value throughout the financing term. Al-Baraka Islamic Bank uses **Takaful insurance** (Islamic insurance) exclusively, in line with our Sharia obligations. The cost of Takaful insurance is borne by the customer and is not included in the quoted monthly installment.

The bank will arrange Takaful coverage through an approved Takaful provider and the annual Takaful cost will be communicated to you at the time of approval. Alternatively, you may arrange your own Takaful coverage through a CBK-licensed Takaful company, subject to the bank's approval of the policy.

For Murabaha and Tawarruq products, insurance is not mandatory but is available upon request.

## Section 8: Early Settlement

All Al-Baraka Islamic Bank financing products allow for early settlement (paying off the remaining balance before the end of the financing term). Under Islamic finance principles, if you settle early, the bank may provide a discretionary reduction in the outstanding profit portion — this is called an **ibra** (rebate). The ibra is not guaranteed and is assessed by the bank on a case-by-case basis in accordance with our Sharia Board's guidelines.

There is no penalty fee for early settlement. The bank will never increase your obligation for choosing to settle early.

For details on the ibra policy, refer to the Sharia Compliance FAQ (ABK-FAQ-001) or contact any branch.

---

## Section 9: Rejected Applications

If your application is declined, you will receive a written notice. Under CBK guidelines, the bank is not required to disclose the specific reasons for a credit decline, but our representatives can provide general guidance on what may have affected your application and what steps you might take to improve your profile for a future application.

You may reapply after a period of 6 months. Reapplying sooner than 6 months without a material change in your circumstances is unlikely to result in a different outcome.
