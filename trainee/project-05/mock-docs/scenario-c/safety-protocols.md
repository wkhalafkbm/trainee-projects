# Safety Protocols — Field Operations

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document defines emergency response and safety protocols for all Kuwait Oil Company (KOC) field sites. All site personnel — including contractors and visitors — must be briefed on the contents of this document during site induction. Compliance is mandatory. In any conflict between this document and an instruction from a local supervisor, the more conservative (safer) course of action applies until the conflict is formally resolved.

---

## 2. Emergency Response Levels

KOC classifies emergencies on a three-level scale. The level determines the response, notifications, and resources mobilised.

### Level 1 — Minor Incident

**Definition:** An event that can be managed by the immediate work party using available resources, with no threat of escalation. Examples: small contained hydrocarbon spill (less than 20 litres, no ignition risk), minor injury requiring basic first aid only, single instrument or utility failure with no operational impact.

**Response:**
- Work party handles the situation using their training and available resources.
- Shift Supervisor is notified by radio or phone within 15 minutes.
- An incident report is submitted in the CMMS within 24 hours.
- No evacuation required.
- Area Authority may choose to suspend nearby work pending assessment.

### Level 2 — Significant Incident

**Definition:** An event that exceeds the capacity of the immediate work party to manage safely, involves a significant release of hazardous material, or has potential to escalate to a major incident. Examples: hydrocarbon gas release (confirmed by LEL detector), fire contained to a single item of equipment, injury requiring more than first aid, H2S detection at or above the IDLH threshold, loss of well control.

**Response:** See Section 3 for first-five-minute actions.

### Level 3 — Major Incident

**Definition:** An event posing an immediate threat to multiple personnel or to site integrity. Examples: large uncontrolled hydrocarbon release or fire, multiple casualties, uncontrolled well blowout, structural collapse.

**Response:**
- Site-wide emergency alarm activated immediately.
- All personnel evacuate to designated muster stations.
- KOC Emergency Response Team (ERT) assumes command.
- Kuwait Civil Defence Authority notified.
- No re-entry to site until ERT declares the all-clear.
- Site Manager and KOC Corporate HSE are notified within 30 minutes.

## 3. First Five Minutes of a Level 2 Incident

The first five minutes of a Level 2 incident are critical. The following steps must be executed in order.

1. **Shout a warning.** Alert everyone within immediate vicinity verbally. Use the phrase: *"Stop work — incident!"* Do not assume others have noticed.

2. **Activate the nearest manual call point** if there is any risk of fire or explosion. Manual call points are the red pull-handle stations located every 50 metres along process areas and at all building exits. This triggers the site alarm and notifies the Control Room automatically.

3. **Move upwind.** Move all personnel in the immediate work party at least 50 metres upwind and uphill of the incident source. Account for all persons — confirm no one is left behind or unaccounted for.

4. **Call the Control Room on radio Channel 3 (Operations).** Give your name, location (well pad number / equipment tag), nature of incident (e.g., "gas release at separator S-204"), number of personnel involved or injured, and your current position. Use clear language — no codes at this stage.

5. **Do not re-enter the hazard zone** for any reason unless you are a trained and equipped member of the Emergency Response Team and have been directed to do so by the Incident Commander.

6. **Provide first aid** at a safe distance if trained to do so and if it is safe to reach the casualty. Do not put additional personnel at risk to retrieve an injured person from an active hazard zone — this is the role of the ERT with appropriate equipment.

7. **Remain at your muster point** until the Shift Supervisor, Area Authority, or Incident Commander releases you. Do not leave the site.

8. **Preserve evidence.** Do not disturb the work site or move equipment unless required to prevent further injury. The Incident Commander will secure the area for investigation.

## 4. Evacuation Procedures

### 4.1 Mustering

Every site personnel member must know their designated muster station before beginning any work. Muster station assignments are given during site induction and are posted at all site entrances and accommodation areas.

1. On hearing the site evacuation alarm (continuous horn), stop all work immediately.
2. Secure any immediate hazards if safe to do so (e.g., close a valve, cap a line) — this must take no more than 30 seconds. If it cannot be done in 30 seconds, leave it.
3. Proceed directly to your designated muster station by the nearest safe route. Do not use internal roads if a vehicle convoy is in progress.
4. Do not collect personal belongings. Do not use lifts (elevators) if present.
5. Report to the muster station warden. Give your name and confirm you are safe.
6. The muster station warden performs a roll call against the site access register.
7. If you cannot reach your designated muster station, go to the nearest alternative muster station and notify the warden there.
8. Remain at the muster station until the all-clear is given by the Site Manager or Incident Commander. The all-clear is communicated over the PA system and by the warden.

### 4.2 Personnel Accountability

Any personnel found not at a muster station after a roll call is treated as a missing person until confirmed otherwise. Search and rescue operations will be initiated for unaccounted personnel. Do not leave the muster station without telling the warden, even after roll call is complete.

## 5. H2S Gas Detection Procedures

Hydrogen sulphide (H2S) is an extremely hazardous gas that occurs naturally in crude oil and gas streams at KOC sites. H2S is colourless and smells strongly of rotten eggs at low concentrations — but critically, at concentrations sufficient to cause unconsciousness, the smell cannot be detected because it paralyses the olfactory nerve. Never rely on smell alone to assess H2S safety.

### 5.1 Personal H2S Monitor

All personnel in field areas must wear a calibrated personal H2S monitor clipped to the upper chest or collar. The monitor must be bump-tested before each shift. Do not enter field areas with an uncalibrated or untested monitor.

### 5.2 H2S Alarm Levels and Response

| Alarm Level | Concentration | Monitor Signal | Required Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warning (Low)** | 5 ppm | Intermittent beep + yellow light | Note the reading. Inform Shift Supervisor. Investigate source if safe. Remain alert. Do not work alone. |
| **Alarm (High)** | 10 ppm | Continuous beep + red light | Stop work immediately. Move upwind. Notify Control Room. Do not re-enter until Shift Supervisor clears the area. |
| **Evacuation** | 20 ppm | Loud continuous alarm + red light | Evacuate area immediately without delay. Move to muster station. Do not attempt to identify or control the source. Notify Control Room by radio from a safe distance. |
| **IDLH** | 50 ppm | Maximum alarm | This is an immediately dangerous to life and health concentration. Treat as Level 2 incident minimum. ERT with SCBA required for any response. |

### 5.3 Responding to an H2S Alarm

1. Do not attempt to identify the H2S source before evacuating. Your life takes priority.
2. Move upwind immediately. H2S is heavier than air — avoid low-lying areas, trenches, and drains.
3. If a colleague is overcome by H2S, do not enter the affected zone without SCBA breathing apparatus. Attempting a rescue without SCBA will result in two casualties. Call the ERT.
4. From a safe location, contact the Control Room on radio Channel 3 to report the alarm, your location, your headcount, and any known casualties.
5. Fixed H2S detectors are installed throughout the site. If a fixed detector alarms, the Control Room will receive an automatic notification and will initiate the appropriate response.

## 6. Fire Response Procedure

1. **Discover a fire:** Shout *"Fire!"* to alert nearby personnel. Do not assume alarms will activate automatically.
2. **Activate the nearest manual call point** immediately — do not wait to assess the fire's size.
3. **Evaluate: can this fire be safely fought?** Only attempt to fight a fire if ALL of the following are true: the fire is small (limited to a single item, not yet spreading), you have a clear escape route behind you, you have the correct extinguisher for the fire type, you have been trained to use a fire extinguisher. If any condition is not met, evacuate.
4. **Select the correct extinguisher:**
   - Class A (paper, wood, general materials): Water or foam
   - Class B (flammable liquids, hydrocarbons): Dry powder or CO2 — NEVER water
   - Class C (electrical): CO2 or dry powder — NEVER water or foam
5. **Use the PASS technique:** Pull the pin — Aim at the base of the fire — Squeeze the handle — Sweep side to side.
6. **If the fire is not extinguished within 30 seconds, stop and evacuate.** Do not keep trying. The fire is escalating.
7. **Evacuate all personnel** from the area and muster per Section 4.
8. **Do not re-enter** the area for any reason until the Shift Supervisor or ERT Commander declares it safe.
9. **Report to the muster station** and give the warden a description of the fire location, type, and size at the time you left.

**Note on hydrocarbon fires:** Crude oil, condensate, and gas fires are classified as Level 2 at minimum and Level 3 if uncontrolled. The ERT and Kuwait Civil Defence Authority will be involved. Under no circumstances should a field engineer attempt to fight a process fire alone.

## 7. Safety Observation Report (SOR)

A Safety Observation Report allows any site personnel to report an unsafe condition, near-miss, or positive safety observation. Submitting SORs is not optional — all personnel are expected to submit a minimum of two SORs per month. Near-miss reporting is treated as a positive safety behaviour.

### 7.1 How to Submit an SOR

**Method 1 — Digital (preferred):**
1. Open the KOC Safety App on your site-issued device or scan the QR code at any site notice board.
2. Select the observation type: Unsafe Condition / Unsafe Act / Near-Miss / Positive Observation.
3. Enter the location (well pad, equipment tag, area description).
4. Describe what you observed in plain language. Be specific: what was the hazard, what could have gone wrong, who was affected.
5. If possible, attach a photograph.
6. Submit. You will receive a reference number.

**Method 2 — Paper form (if digital is unavailable):**
1. Obtain an SOR paper form from the site office or Control Room.
2. Complete all fields: date, time, location, observer name and employee number, observation type, description.
3. Hand the completed form to the Shift Supervisor or drop it in the SOR box at the site office.
4. The Shift Supervisor will enter it into the digital system within 24 hours and provide you with a reference number.

### 7.2 What Happens After Submission

- The HSE team reviews all SORs within 48 hours.
- Urgent safety conditions (imminent risk of injury) are escalated to the Area Authority for same-day action.
- The submitter receives feedback within 7 working days on what action, if any, was taken.
- All SORs are aggregated monthly to identify site-wide safety trends.
- Anonymous submission is available through the digital system. Note that anonymous reports receive the same investigation priority, but feedback cannot be sent to the submitter.

## 8. Working in Extreme Heat

Kuwait experiences extreme summer temperatures. Outdoor working conditions between May and September can reach 50°C or above. Heat-related illness (heat exhaustion, heat stroke) is a genuine life-threatening risk. The following procedures are mandatory and enforced during the summer operating season (1 May to 30 September). Supervisors who permit non-compliant work during this period are personally accountable.

### 8.1 Maximum Outdoor Work Duration by Temperature

| Ambient Temperature | Maximum Continuous Outdoor Work | Mandatory Rest Period |
|---|---|---|
| Below 40°C | 60 minutes | 15 minutes in shaded or cooled area |
| 40°C to 44°C | 45 minutes | 20 minutes in shaded or cooled area |
| 45°C to 49°C | 30 minutes | 30 minutes in air-conditioned area |
| 50°C and above | 20 minutes | 40 minutes in air-conditioned area |

Temperature is measured using the site's official weather station readings, posted hourly on the site notice board and the KOC Operations App. Personal temperature readings are not accepted as the basis for deviation from these limits.

Work in direct sunlight adds an effective 3–5°C to the thermal load. When working in direct sunlight, apply the thresholds for the next higher temperature band.

### 8.2 Mandatory Rest and Hydration Requirements

1. **Hydration:** Drink a minimum of 250 ml of water every 20 minutes during outdoor work. Water stations are located at every well pad access gate and every 100 metres along main process corridors. Cold water and oral rehydration salts (ORS) are provided. Do not wait until you feel thirsty — thirst is a late indicator of dehydration.
2. **No caffeine before or during hot work:** Caffeine and energy drinks are prohibited during outdoor work shifts in the summer season. They accelerate dehydration.
3. **Pre-shift health check:** Personnel must not begin outdoor work if they have experienced any of the following in the previous 24 hours: vomiting, diarrhoea, fever, or poor sleep exceeding one night. Notify the Shift Supervisor before your shift if any of these apply.
4. **Buddy system:** Work alone is prohibited during outdoor activities when the temperature exceeds 45°C. A second person must be present and capable of calling for help.
5. **Cooling areas:** Designated cooling areas (air-conditioned site containers) are marked on the site map distributed at induction. These must remain unlocked and operational at all times during the summer season.

### 8.3 Recognising and Responding to Heat Illness

| Condition | Symptoms | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Heat cramps | Muscle spasms, sweating | Move to cool area, rest, drink ORS, do not return to work until spasms resolve |
| Heat exhaustion | Heavy sweating, weakness, pale skin, nausea, headache, dizziness | Move to cool area immediately, apply cool wet cloths, give water, notify Shift Supervisor, do not return to work that shift |
| Heat stroke | Hot dry skin or heavy sweating, confusion, loss of coordination, altered consciousness | MEDICAL EMERGENCY — call Control Room immediately, apply cool water to entire body, fan aggressively, do not leave the person alone — this is a Level 1 minimum incident |

Any personnel suspected of heat stroke must be evacuated to medical facilities immediately. Do not allow them to return to work.
