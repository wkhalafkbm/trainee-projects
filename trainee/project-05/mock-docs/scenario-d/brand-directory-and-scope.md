# Group Brand Directory and Customer Care Scope

**Document:** Group Customer Care — Brand Reference Guide
**Applies To:** Contact Centre Agents, Digital Customer Care Assistant, Brand Service Teams
**Version:** 3.1
**Effective Date:** January 2025
**Issuing Department:** Group Customer Experience Office

---

## Purpose

This guide defines every brand operating under the Group's Automotive, Electronics, and Consumer Retail divisions, the products and services each brand covers, and which policy documents apply to which brand. Customer-facing staff and the digital assistant must confirm which brand a customer's enquiry relates to **before** retrieving or quoting any warranty, service, or pricing information. Quoting one brand's terms in response to a query about a different brand is treated as a serious service failure, not a minor error — brand terms are not interchangeable even where the underlying policy language looks similar.

## Group Structure

The Group operates three divisions. Each division contains multiple brands. No brand shares warranty or service terms with another brand, even within the same division.

## Brand Identification Rules for Customer Care

Before answering any warranty, service, or policy question, identify:

1. **Which brand** the customer's product or purchase belongs to
2. **Which division's policy set applies** — division-level defaults exist, but individual brands may override them (see `warranty-and-service-policies.md`)
3. Whether the enquiry involves **more than one brand** (e.g. a customer asking about both a Toyota vehicle and a Yiaco appliance in the same message) — treat this as two separate enquiries internally, each resolved against its own brand's policy, never blended into a single answer

**If the brand cannot be confidently identified from the customer's message**, ask a clarifying question before retrieving any policy content. Do not guess based on the product category alone — Yiaco Electronics and Yiaco Mobile are separate brands with separate warranty terms despite the shared "Yiaco" name, and this is a common source of confusion.

## Common Brand Confusion Cases

These cases are deliberately similar-sounding and are the most frequent source of cross-brand errors. Staff and the assistant must treat them as distinct at all times:

- **"Yiaco" ambiguity:** A customer saying "I bought it from Yiaco" could mean Yiaco Electronics (appliances) or Yiaco Mobile (phones/tablets). These have different warranty periods and different service locations. Always confirm the specific product category.
- **Toyota vs. Lexus:** Both are serviced by Group Motors, and some customers refer to "the Toyota centre" when they mean the shared Shuwaikh site that also services Lexus. Vehicle warranty terms differ between the two brands — confirm the vehicle brand from the registration or purchase details, not the service location alone.
- **HomeStyle Retail vs. Group Fashion Outlets:** Both are Consumer Retail division, both handle returns in-store only, but return windows differ (see `warranty-and-service-policies.md`). A generic "consumer retail return policy" does not exist — always resolve to the specific brand.

## Escalation Contacts by Brand

| Brand | Escalation Team | When to Escalate |
|---|---|---|
| Toyota / Lexus (Group Motors) | Group Motors Customer Relations | Any warranty dispute, any request involving vehicle safety recalls |
| FleetCare Commercial | Commercial Accounts Team | Any request outside a standing fleet contract |
| Yiaco Electronics | Yiaco Electronics Service Desk | Repair disputes, extended warranty claims |
| Yiaco Mobile | Yiaco Mobile Support | Device replacement disputes, insurance claims |
| HomeStyle Retail | HomeStyle Customer Care | Delivery damage disputes, large order issues |
| Group Fashion Outlets | Store-level Duty Manager | All disputes — Fashion Outlets has no centralised escalation team |

Hand-offs to any escalation team must include: the brand identified, the product/order details already provided by the customer, and a summary of what has already been discussed — the customer should never be asked to repeat information already given to the assistant.
