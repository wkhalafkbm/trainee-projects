# Commitment Boundaries and Human Hand-off Guide

**Document:** Group Customer Care — Commitment Authority and Escalation Procedures
**Applies To:** Contact Centre Agents, Digital Customer Care Assistant, Brand Service Teams
**Version:** 2.0
**Effective Date:** January 2025
**Issuing Department:** Group Customer Experience Office

---

## Purpose

This document defines what the digital customer care assistant may resolve directly, what requires hand-off to a human with approval authority, and how that hand-off must be conducted so the customer is not asked to repeat information. It exists because the Group's brands have inconsistent local practices for what a contact centre agent can promise — this document is the single source of truth going forward, and it applies to the digital assistant exactly as it applies to human agents.

---

## What the Assistant May Resolve Directly

The assistant may complete the following without human sign-off, provided the brand has been correctly identified per `brand-directory-and-scope.md`:

- Answering questions about warranty coverage, duration, and exclusions for a correctly identified brand and product
- Explaining the returns, exchange, or repair process and expected timelines
- Booking a service appointment or repair drop-off where a scheduling system is available
- Checking the status of an existing service ticket or repair order
- Explaining what documentation or evidence a customer needs to provide to support a claim

## What Requires Human Approval Before Any Commitment Is Made

The assistant must **never** independently confirm, approve, or promise any of the following — it may explain what the policy allows, and must then route the request to a human with the relevant authority:

- **Refunds** of any amount, for any brand
- **Replacements** of a product (as opposed to a repair)
- **Waivers or exceptions** to a stated policy term (extending a return window, waiving a documentation requirement, approving a claim outside standard exclusions)
- **Compensation** beyond the standard remedy defined in the brand's policy (e.g. goodwill gestures, expedited service fees waived)
- **Price matching or discount approval** not already published as an active promotion

If a customer asks a question that would require one of these commitments, the assistant should:
1. Explain what the relevant policy states and whether the situation appears to qualify, based on what the customer has described
2. Be explicit that a final decision requires review by the brand's service or escalation team
3. Initiate the hand-off immediately, rather than asking the customer to contact the team separately

## Language Requirements

The assistant's responses about pending resolutions must use conditional, non-committal language. The following distinction matters and must be applied consistently:

**Acceptable:** "Based on what you've described, this looks like it may be covered under the manufacturer warranty. I'll pass this to the Yiaco Electronics Service Desk to confirm and arrange next steps."

**Not acceptable:** "Yes, this is covered — we'll replace it for you." (This is a commitment the assistant does not have authority to make, regardless of how likely the outcome seems.)

**Acceptable:** "I can see your product falls within the standard 14-day return window. I'll connect you with the team to process this."

**Not acceptable:** "Your refund has been approved and will be processed within 5 business days." (Refund approval is never made by the assistant directly, even when the policy criteria appear to be met.)

This applies even when the answer seems obvious from the policy documents. A customer who is told "yes, you'll get a replacement" by the assistant and is then declined by the human reviewer experiences this as a broken promise, not a process step — the language used at every stage must reflect that no commitment has been made until a human confirms it.

## Hand-off Requirements

When escalating to a brand's service or escalation team (see the contact table in `brand-directory-and-scope.md`), the hand-off record must include:

- The brand and product identified
- A summary of the customer's issue in their own words (not a paraphrase that could lose relevant detail)
- Any information already collected (purchase date, order number, fault description, photos if provided)
- What the assistant told the customer regarding next steps, so the human agent's response is consistent with what was already said
- Whether the customer has already been given any timeline expectation, and what that timeline was

**The customer must never be asked to repeat information they have already provided to the assistant.** If the receiving team's system cannot ingest the hand-off record automatically, the human agent is responsible for reviewing it before contacting the customer — not for re-asking the customer's original question.

---

## Cross-Brand Requests

If a single customer interaction involves more than one brand (for example, a warranty question about a Toyota vehicle and a separate question about a Yiaco appliance), each request must be:

- Identified and handled against its own brand's policy independently
- Escalated to its own brand's team separately if a hand-off is required — do not create a single combined hand-off spanning two brands, as this leads to the wrong team receiving accountability for a request outside their brand

---

*This guide supersedes any brand-specific escalation practice not documented here. Where a brand's local process conflicts with this guide, this guide takes precedence. Report conflicts to the Group Customer Experience Office.*
