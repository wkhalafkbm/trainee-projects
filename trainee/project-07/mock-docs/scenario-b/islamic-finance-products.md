## How Islamic Finance Differs from Conventional Lending

Before describing individual products, it helps to understand the principles that shape all Islamic finance products.

### No Interest (Riba)

In Islamic finance, charging or paying interest (known as **riba**) is prohibited. Riba is considered exploitative because it creates a guaranteed profit for the lender regardless of the outcome for the borrower. In conventional banking, a loan charges you a percentage of the borrowed amount as interest — you pay more the longer it takes you to repay, regardless of how the underlying situation changes.

Islamic finance products do not charge interest. Instead, the bank either earns a profit through buying and selling an asset at a disclosed markup (Murabaha), earns rental income from an asset it owns and leases to you (Ijara), or participates in a structured commodity transaction (Tawarruq). In every case, the bank's return is tied to a real asset and a real transaction — not to the passage of time on a loan.

### Asset-Backed Transactions

Every Islamic finance transaction must be connected to a real, tangible asset or a legitimate commercial activity. You cannot receive money simply in exchange for a promise to repay more money later. The asset gives the transaction substance and legitimacy in Sharia terms.

### Sharia Board Oversight

Al-Baraka Islamic Bank's Sharia Supervisory Board is composed of recognized Islamic scholars with expertise in both Islamic jurisprudence (fiqh) and modern finance. The Board:
- Reviews and approves every new product before it is offered to customers
- Audits existing products annually to confirm ongoing compliance
- Issues fatwas (scholarly rulings) on novel or complex financial questions
- Reviews complaints related to Sharia matters

The Board operates independently of the bank's management. Its members are not employees of the bank. This independence is essential to ensuring that our products remain genuinely Sharia-compliant and are not merely conventional products with Islamic labels.

### Transparency

In an Islamic finance contract, the bank's profit margin must be disclosed upfront. You will always know the total amount you will pay before you sign. There are no hidden fees or variable charges that could increase your obligation unexpectedly.

## Product 1: Murabaha (Cost-Plus Financing)

### What Is Murabaha?

Murabaha is a straightforward purchase-and-resale arrangement. You want to buy something — a car, a piece of furniture, a laptop, a household appliance. You do not have the cash to pay for it today. Instead of the bank giving you a loan and charging interest, the bank buys the item for you at the market price, and then sells it to you at a higher price that includes the bank's disclosed profit. You repay the bank in installments over an agreed period.

The key point: the bank genuinely purchases the item before selling it to you. For a brief moment, the bank owns the asset. This real ownership — even if it lasts only minutes — is what makes the transaction permissible under Sharia. The bank is earning a profit from trade, not from lending money.

### How It Works: Step by Step

1. You identify the item you wish to purchase (e.g., a specific car model from a specific dealership).
2. You apply to the bank for Murabaha financing, specifying the item and its price.
3. The bank reviews your application and, if approved, purchases the item directly from the supplier at the market price.
4. The bank then sells the item to you at the original price plus a disclosed profit margin, agreed before any contract is signed.
5. You take ownership of the item immediately and repay the bank in monthly installments over the agreed period (typically 12 to 60 months).
6. The total amount you pay — the original cost plus the bank's profit — is fixed from day one and does not change.

### What Is Murabaha Commonly Used For?

- **Vehicle purchase:** New and used cars, motorcycles, and commercial vehicles
- **Consumer electronics:** Laptops, televisions, home appliances
- **Furniture and home furnishings**
- **Personal equipment:** Medical equipment, tools, professional instruments

Murabaha is not typically used for large purchases like real estate (Ijara or other structures are more suitable for property) or for general cash needs (Tawarruq is more appropriate for that).

### Worked Example: Vehicle Murabaha

**Scenario:** Ahmed wants to buy a new car priced at KWD 8,000. He applies to Al-Baraka for Murabaha financing.

- **Car price (cost to bank):** KWD 8,000
- **Bank's profit margin:** 12% over 3 years (disclosed upfront)
- **Bank's total profit:** KWD 960
- **Total amount Ahmed pays:** KWD 8,960
- **Monthly installment:** KWD 248.89 over 36 months

From the moment the contract is signed, Ahmed knows he will pay exactly KWD 8,960 in total. This amount will not increase if he takes longer to pay (though early settlement options are available — see the FAQ). There are no surprise charges.

### Who Is Murabaha Suitable For?

Murabaha is suitable for customers who:
- Want to purchase a specific, identifiable item
- Prefer predictable fixed monthly payments
- Want to own the item immediately upon purchase
- Are looking to finance goods rather than real estate or general cash needs

## Product 2: Ijara (Lease-to-Own)

### What Is Ijara?

Ijara means "lease" in Arabic. In an Ijara arrangement, the bank purchases an asset (typically a property or a large piece of equipment) and then leases it to you for an agreed rental period. At the end of the lease, ownership of the asset is transferred to you — usually for a nominal payment or as a gift.

Because the bank owns the asset throughout the lease period, it bears certain ownership responsibilities (for example, the risk of major structural damage to a property is the bank's responsibility, not the tenant's — though day-to-day maintenance is typically the lessee's responsibility). This genuine ownership risk is what distinguishes Ijara from conventional mortgage financing, where the lender has a security interest but the borrower bears all ownership risk from day one.

The term "Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek" refers to the lease-with-ownership-transfer structure — the most common form used in retail banking.

### How It Works: Step by Step

1. You identify the property or asset you wish to eventually own (e.g., an apartment or a piece of industrial machinery).
2. You apply to the bank for Ijara financing.
3. The bank purchases the asset from the seller at the agreed market price.
4. The bank leases the asset to you for an agreed rental period (typically 5 to 25 years for property; shorter for equipment).
5. You pay monthly rental installments. A portion of each payment reduces the bank's ownership stake; you gradually build your ownership share.
6. At the end of the lease term, the bank transfers full ownership to you — typically for a nominal KWD 1 payment or as a gift recorded in the contract.

### What Is Ijara Commonly Used For?

- **Residential property purchase:** Apartments and villas for personal use
- **Commercial property acquisition:** Offices, warehouses, retail units
- **Large equipment financing:** Medical equipment, industrial machinery, agricultural equipment
- **Vehicle fleet financing:** For companies financing a fleet of vehicles

### Worked Example: Residential Property Ijara

**Scenario:** Fatima wants to purchase an apartment priced at KWD 120,000. She applies for Ijara financing with a 20-year term.

- **Property price (purchased by bank):** KWD 120,000
- **Customer's initial contribution (down payment):** KWD 24,000 (20%)
- **Amount financed by bank:** KWD 96,000
- **Bank's rental profit (over 20 years):** KWD 48,000 (disclosed upfront)
- **Total Fatima pays over 20 years:** KWD 144,000 (KWD 96,000 principal + KWD 48,000 rental profit)
- **Monthly installment:** KWD 600

Fatima moves into the apartment immediately and lives there as a tenant-owner. If the building needs a major structural repair, the bank (as legal owner) is responsible. Fatima is responsible for utilities and regular upkeep. At the end of year 20, the apartment is legally transferred to Fatima for a symbolic fee.

### Who Is Ijara Suitable For?

Ijara is suitable for customers who:
- Wish to purchase property or large assets
- Are comfortable with the lease-then-own structure
- Can provide an initial down payment (minimum 20% for residential property — see Eligibility Guide)
- Are planning a long-term commitment (Ijara contracts are not easily restructured once signed)

## Product 3: Tawarruq (Commodity-Backed Personal Finance)

### What Is Tawarruq?

Tawarruq is a more complex structure, but its purpose is simple: to provide a customer with liquid cash for general personal needs, in a Sharia-compliant way.

In conventional banking, this would simply be a personal loan: the bank gives you money, you promise to repay more money later. In Islamic finance, providing money in exchange for more money is not permissible (that is the definition of riba). Tawarruq creates a legitimate structure by routing the transaction through a real commodity market.

Here is what happens: the bank buys a commodity (typically base metals traded on the London Metal Exchange or a similar recognized commodity market) on your behalf. The bank then sells that commodity to you on credit — meaning you owe the bank the commodity's price on a deferred basis. You then immediately sell that commodity to a third party (a market participant) for cash at today's price. The cash is yours to use for whatever you need. You repay the bank the deferred price in monthly installments.

The result is economically similar to a conventional personal loan, but structurally it involves real commodity transactions that give it Sharia legitimacy.

### How It Works: Step by Step

1. You apply for Tawarruq financing and specify the amount of cash you need.
2. The bank purchases a commodity valued at approximately the required amount from the commodity market.
3. The bank sells the commodity to you at a higher price (cost + profit), payable in deferred installments over an agreed period.
4. You immediately sell the commodity to a third-party buyer in the market for the current market price (cash today).
5. You receive the cash proceeds from the commodity sale.
6. You repay the bank in monthly installments at the agreed deferred price.

In practice, the commodity transactions (steps 2–4) happen electronically and nearly simultaneously. You do not physically handle any metal. The bank's treasury desk manages the commodity market transactions on your behalf.

### What Is Tawarruq Commonly Used For?

- **Personal cash needs:** Medical expenses, education costs, home renovation, family events
- **Debt consolidation:** Paying off obligations from other sources
- **Business working capital:** For small business owners who need short-term liquidity
- **Emergency funds**

Tawarruq is specifically designed for situations where you need cash, not a specific asset. If you are buying a car, use Murabaha. If you are buying a home, use Ijara. If you simply need money for general purposes, Tawarruq is the appropriate product.

### Worked Example: Personal Tawarruq

**Scenario:** Khaled needs KWD 5,000 for home renovation. He applies for Tawarruq financing over 24 months.

- **Cash required:** KWD 5,000
- **Commodity purchased by bank (at market price):** KWD 5,000 worth of aluminum
- **Bank sells commodity to Khaled (deferred price):** KWD 5,600 (KWD 5,000 + KWD 600 profit margin)
- **Khaled sells commodity to third-party buyer:** Receives KWD 5,000 cash
- **Khaled's monthly installment:** KWD 233.33 over 24 months
- **Total Khaled pays:** KWD 5,600

Khaled now has KWD 5,000 to fund his renovation. He repays KWD 233.33 per month for two years.

### Who Is Tawarruq Suitable For?

Tawarruq is suitable for customers who:
- Need liquid cash for general personal or family needs
- Do not have a specific asset they wish to purchase
- Have a stable income sufficient to support the monthly installment
- Understand that the financing is backed by a commodity transaction structure
