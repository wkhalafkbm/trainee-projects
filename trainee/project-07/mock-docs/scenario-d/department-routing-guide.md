## Important Notice to Triage Personnel

This guide exists to help triage staff route patients efficiently to the correct department. It is a routing tool, not a clinical decision-making tool. Triage personnel must not use this document to:

- **Diagnose** a patient's condition
- **Recommend or suggest medication** of any kind
- **Tell a patient that their complaint is not serious**
- **Advise a patient to go home or to a pharmacy** without consulting a triage nurse or physician
- **Make assumptions about acuity** based on a patient's appearance, age, or manner

If you are uncertain where to route a patient, or if the patient's complaint does not match any routing rule in this guide, stop and refer the case to the senior triage nurse immediately. It is always better to over-route (send a patient to a more specialised department) than to under-route.

---

## Overriding Rule — Always Route to Emergency

**Regardless of any other factor, any patient presenting with symptoms that may indicate the following conditions must be routed to the Emergency Department immediately. Do not route to a specialty department. Do not ask further intake questions first. Alert the Emergency triage nurse at once.**

- Chest pain or pressure
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath at rest
- Signs of a possible stroke: sudden facial drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty, sudden severe headache (use the FAST acronym — Face, Arms, Speech, Time)
- Loss of consciousness or unresponsiveness (including brief loss of consciousness in the past hour)
- Severe uncontrolled bleeding
- Major trauma: fall from height, road traffic accident, penetrating injury
- Suspected poisoning or overdose
- Seizure occurring now or within the past 30 minutes
- Signs of severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis): swelling of throat or tongue, hives spreading rapidly, difficulty swallowing

If in any doubt about whether a symptom falls under this rule, treat it as if it does and route to Emergency.

## 1. Emergency Department

### What this department handles
Life-threatening and time-sensitive conditions requiring immediate physician assessment, resuscitation capability, or advanced diagnostics. All of the conditions listed in the Overriding Rule above. Multi-system trauma. Altered mental status. Acute severe pain of undetermined cause.

### Urgent routing (patient must be taken there immediately)
Anything in the Overriding Rule. Additionally: sudden onset of severe abdominal pain, high fever with altered consciousness, suspected meningitis (severe headache, neck stiffness, light sensitivity), acute vision loss, diabetic emergencies (hypoglycaemia with confusion, DKA).

### Routine routing
Not applicable — Emergency does not have a routine intake pathway. Patients who are stable and do not meet urgent criteria may be directed to other departments after the triage nurse assesses them.

### Information the receiving nurse needs
- Chief complaint (in patient's own words)
- Time of onset
- Brief vital signs if taken at intake (pulse, SpO2, blood pressure)
- Any known relevant medical history provided by the patient (e.g., "patient states they are diabetic")
- Whether the patient arrived alone or with family/companion

## 2. Cardiology Department

### What this department handles
Non-emergency cardiac conditions: known or suspected arrhythmias, follow-up after cardiac events, management of stable heart failure, hypertension management, pre-operative cardiac assessment, palpitations, and investigation of cardiac risk factors.

### Urgent routing (within 30 minutes of arrival)
New palpitations with dizziness but no loss of consciousness. Syncope (fainting episode) that has now resolved, with no current symptoms — these patients should first be seen by Emergency if the syncope was cardiac in nature; route to Cardiology only if Emergency has cleared them. Known arrhythmia patient reporting an increase in symptoms.

**Note:** Any patient with chest pain, severe shortness of breath, or ongoing dizziness with suspected cardiac cause routes to Emergency first, not Cardiology.

### Routine routing
Scheduled follow-up appointments. Patients referred by GP or Internal Medicine for cardiac investigation. Hypertension review.

### Information the receiving nurse needs
- Symptom description and duration
- Known cardiac diagnoses and current cardiac medications
- Any recent ECG results the patient has with them
- Whether this is a new symptom or a change from baseline

---

## 3. Orthopaedics Department

### What this department handles
Injuries and conditions affecting bones, joints, muscles, and ligaments. Fractures, dislocations, joint pain, back and spine conditions, sports injuries, post-operative orthopaedic follow-up.

### Urgent routing (within 30 minutes)
Suspected fracture with significant deformity, swelling, or inability to bear weight. Joint dislocation. Acute severe back pain with any neurological symptom (leg weakness, numbness, or bladder/bowel changes — this may indicate spinal cord involvement and is a medical emergency: route to Emergency first, then notify Orthopaedics).

**Note:** Trauma mechanism injuries (high-speed road accident, fall from height) route to Emergency first regardless of the apparent injury type.

### Routine routing
Chronic joint pain. Scheduled orthopaedic appointments. Minor sprains without neurovascular compromise. Post-operative wound checks.

### Information the receiving nurse needs
- Mechanism of injury (how did it happen)
- Affected body part
- Ability to bear weight / use the affected limb
- Time of injury
- Any imaging the patient has brought

## 4. Paediatrics Department

### What this department handles
Medical conditions in patients under 18 years of age. Fever in children, respiratory infections, gastrointestinal illness, developmental concerns, immunisations, and general paediatric follow-up.

**Important:** Children under 12 years with any concerning symptom should be routed to Paediatrics for assessment. Do not route a child directly to an adult specialty department without Paediatrics input, unless Emergency criteria are met.

### Urgent routing (within 30 minutes)
High fever in a child under 3 months (any fever in a newborn under 28 days is Emergency). Difficulty breathing in a child. Rash with fever. Severe or prolonged vomiting/diarrhoea with signs of dehydration. Seizure (route to Emergency if seizure is ongoing). A child who appears unusually lethargic or unresponsive for their age.

### Routine routing
Scheduled check-ups, immunisations, mild upper respiratory illness in a child who is alert and feeding normally, follow-up visits.

### Information the receiving nurse needs
- Patient's date of birth and weight if known
- Chief complaint
- Duration and onset of symptoms
- Fever temperature if measured at home
- Name of parent or guardian accompanying the child
- Any known allergies (especially medication allergies)

## 5. Obstetrics and Gynaecology (O&G) Department

### What this department handles
Conditions related to pregnancy, childbirth, and the female reproductive system. Antenatal and postnatal care, gynaecological conditions, menstrual disorders, and contraception consultations.

### Urgent routing (within 30 minutes)
Active labour or suspected labour. Heavy vaginal bleeding in a pregnant patient (route to Emergency if bleeding is severe or patient is haemodynamically unstable). Sudden severe abdominal pain in a pregnant patient. Reduced fetal movement reported by the patient. Signs of pre-eclampsia in a known pregnant patient: severe headache, visual disturbance, swelling of face and hands. Suspected ectopic pregnancy: severe one-sided lower abdominal pain with missed period.

**Note:** Severe pain with haemodynamic instability in a pregnant patient routes to Emergency first.

### Routine routing
Scheduled antenatal appointments, postnatal check-ups, smear tests, routine gynaecological consultations.

### Information the receiving nurse needs
- Gestational age (weeks) if pregnant
- Gravida and parity (number of pregnancies and deliveries) if available
- Nature of the presenting complaint
- Last menstrual period date
- Whether the patient's obstetrician is within this hospital

## 6. General Surgery Department

### What this department handles
Surgical assessment and management of abdominal conditions, hernias, gallbladder disease, appendicitis, colorectal conditions, and non-cardiac chest wall conditions. Pre-operative and post-operative surgical care.

### Urgent routing (within 30 minutes)
Suspected appendicitis: severe right lower abdominal pain, fever, nausea. Acute abdomen: severe diffuse abdominal pain, rigidity, guarding. Post-operative patient with fever, increasing pain, wound breakdown, or signs of infection. Suspected bowel obstruction: abdominal distension, no bowel movements, vomiting.

**Note:** Suspected perforated abdominal organ or signs of peritonitis (rigid abdomen, high fever, extreme pain) route to Emergency first.

### Routine routing
Hernia consultations, outpatient surgical follow-up, minor skin lesion removal, elective surgical consultations.

### Information the receiving nurse needs
- Location and character of pain
- Duration of symptoms
- Last bowel movement
- Any recent surgical history
- Current temperature if measured

---

## 7. Internal Medicine Department

### What this department handles
Complex or multi-system medical conditions not primarily surgical or cardiac. Diabetes management, respiratory conditions (non-emergency), thyroid disorders, infectious disease, kidney conditions, anaemia, and general adult medicine.

### Urgent routing (within 30 minutes)
Suspected diabetic emergency that is stable (patient is alert and able to follow commands — if not alert, route to Emergency). New neurological symptom in a diabetic patient. Suspected severe infection in an immunocompromised patient. Acute kidney injury: reduced urine output, generalised swelling, known kidney disease with new symptoms.

### Routine routing
Chronic disease management follow-up, annual health reviews, management of known conditions (diabetes, hypertension, thyroid), investigation of fatigue, weight loss, or anaemia.

### Information the receiving nurse needs
- Primary complaint
- Relevant chronic conditions
- Current medication list (including insulin, anticoagulants, immunosuppressants)
- Last relevant blood test results if patient has them
- GP referral letter if applicable

---

## 8. Psychiatry Department

### What this department handles
Mental health conditions: depression, anxiety, psychosis, bipolar disorder, eating disorders, substance use disorders, acute psychiatric crisis, and psychiatric medication management.

### Urgent routing (within 30 minutes)
Any patient expressing thoughts of self-harm or suicide. Any patient in acute psychotic crisis (severely disorganised behaviour, hallucinations, inability to communicate coherently). Any patient brought in by police or family due to a mental health crisis. Acute panic attack with cardiovascular symptoms should be assessed by Emergency first to exclude cardiac cause before routing to Psychiatry.

**Important:** Do not leave a patient who has expressed suicidal ideation alone in a waiting area. Notify the senior triage nurse immediately and ensure the patient is accompanied at all times until assessed.

### Routine routing
Scheduled psychiatric appointments, medication review, psychological therapy referrals.

### Information the receiving nurse needs
- Presenting concern (use the patient's own words where possible)
- Whether the patient is expressing any thoughts of self-harm or harm to others
- Known psychiatric diagnoses and current psychiatric medications
- Name of their psychiatrist or mental health team if known
- Whether they came voluntarily or were accompanied

## Summary Routing Quick Reference

| Presenting Symptom / Condition | Primary Department | Emergency Override? |
|---|---|---|
| Chest pain (any) | Emergency | Yes — always |
| Difficulty breathing at rest | Emergency | Yes — always |
| Stroke symptoms (FAST) | Emergency | Yes — always |
| Loss of consciousness | Emergency | Yes — always |
| Severe bleeding | Emergency | Yes — always |
| Palpitations, no LOC, stable | Cardiology | No |
| Fracture / dislocation | Orthopaedics | Only if trauma mechanism |
| Child with fever | Paediatrics | Only if very young / altered state |
| Active labour | O&G | Only if unstable |
| Abdominal pain, suspected appendix | General Surgery | Only if peritonitis signs |
| Diabetes management | Internal Medicine | Only if altered consciousness |
| Suicidal ideation | Psychiatry (with escort) | If immediate attempt risk |
