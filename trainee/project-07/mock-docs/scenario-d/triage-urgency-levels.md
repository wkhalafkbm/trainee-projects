# Triage Urgency Classification Guide
**Document ID:** HAL-TRIAGE-URGENCY-002
**Issued by:** Al-Hilal Private Hospital — Nursing and Clinical Operations
**Revision:** 2.4
**Last Updated:** March 2026
**Authorised by:** Director of Nursing; Medical Director

## Purpose

This guide defines the four urgency levels used by Al-Hilal Hospital's triage system. It specifies which presenting complaints correspond to each level, what action the system takes when a level is assigned, and who is notified. Every patient who arrives at the hospital must receive an urgency classification before any administrative intake (insurance, registration) proceeds.

**A patient's urgency level may be escalated at any time as new information becomes available. It may never be downgraded without assessment by a nurse or physician.**

---

## Automatic Escalation Rule

Regardless of other information gathered, the following symptoms automatically assign a classification of **Immediate** when reported by or observed in the patient. No further information is needed to assign this level — do not delay to gather more history first.

- **Chest pain or pressure** of any kind, including mild chest discomfort
- **Difficulty breathing** at rest or with minimal exertion
- **Loss of consciousness**, including brief episodes in the past 60 minutes
- **Severe or uncontrolled bleeding** — defined as blood-soaked dressings, blood pooling, or arterial bleeding
- **Symptoms of stroke**: sudden facial droop, arm weakness, speech slurring, sudden severe headache (use FAST)
- **Anaphylaxis signs**: throat swelling, rapidly spreading hives, difficulty swallowing or breathing after exposure to a potential allergen

When any of these symptoms are present, assign **Immediate**, notify the Emergency duty nurse verbally, and ensure the patient is accompanied to the Emergency area. Do not send the patient to wait alone.

## Level 1 — Immediate

**Definition:** The patient has a potentially life-threatening condition. Delay in assessment and treatment is likely to result in death or permanent serious harm.

**Time to first physician assessment:** Within 15 minutes of arrival. Ideally within 5 minutes.

### Examples of Immediate Presentations

- Active chest pain, pressure, or tightness
- Active difficulty breathing or oxygen saturation below 90% (if measured)
- Unconscious or barely responsive patient
- Active seizure
- Major trauma (road accident, fall from height, penetrating wound)
- Severe bleeding that is not controlled
- Signs of stroke (FAST positive)
- Suspected anaphylaxis
- Child under 12 months with any of: fever above 38°C, difficulty breathing, poor feeding, altered responsiveness
- Patient brought in by ambulance or emergency services
- Suspected overdose with altered consciousness
- Pregnant patient with heavy vaginal bleeding or severe abdominal pain

### System Action

1. Immediate flag raised in the patient management system — all assigned staff receive a visual and audio alert.
2. Emergency duty nurse is notified verbally in person (not by messaging system — verbal notification is mandatory).
3. Patient is taken directly to the Emergency treatment area — they do not proceed to a waiting area.
4. Resuscitation equipment and crash trolley are confirmed available.
5. Duty physician is paged.

### Who Is Notified

- Emergency duty nurse (verbal, immediate)
- On-call physician (page, within 2 minutes)
- If the patient is a minor: paediatric nurse or on-call paediatrician (simultaneous notification)
- If capacity is at risk: Charge Nurse / Bed Manager

## Level 2 — Urgent

**Definition:** The patient has a serious condition that is currently stable but requires assessment and treatment within 30 minutes. Delay significantly increases the risk of deterioration.

**Time to first physician or senior nurse assessment:** Within 30 minutes of arrival.

### Examples of Urgent Presentations

- Moderate chest pain that has now partially resolved (note: any active chest pain = Immediate)
- Shortness of breath that is present but not severe — patient can speak in full sentences
- Suspected fracture with intact circulation and sensation
- Severe abdominal pain (not currently vomiting blood, no peritoneal signs observed)
- High fever in an adult (above 39°C) with rigors
- Acute onset severe headache — "worst headache of my life" or thunderclap headache
- Altered mental status that is mild (confused but responsive)
- Severe pain (patient self-reports 8/10 or above on pain scale)
- Vaginal bleeding in a pregnant patient (stable observations)
- Child under 5 with high fever above 39.5°C and any additional symptom
- Suicidal ideation (active) with no current attempt

### System Action

1. Urgent flag raised in the patient management system.
2. Triage nurse assigns the patient to a waiting area with direct line of sight to the nursing station.
3. Nurse performs an abbreviated assessment including at minimum: pulse, blood pressure, respiratory rate, temperature, SpO2.
4. Patient is reassessed every 10 minutes while waiting.
5. If condition changes while waiting, the urgency level is escalated immediately.

### Who Is Notified

- Receiving department senior nurse or duty physician (within 5 minutes of level assignment)
- Charge Nurse if Emergency is at capacity
- For paediatric Urgent patients under age 5: paediatric nurse notified (see Paediatric section below)

## Level 3 — Semi-Urgent

**Definition:** The patient needs medical attention today, but their condition is stable and is unlikely to deteriorate within the next 1–2 hours.

**Time to first assessment:** Within 2 hours of arrival.

### Examples of Semi-Urgent Presentations

- Moderate pain (5–7 out of 10 on pain scale) that is stable and not worsening
- Urinary tract infection symptoms: burning, frequency, lower abdominal discomfort
- Minor lacerations requiring suturing but with controlled bleeding
- Uncomplicated vomiting or diarrhoea in an adult with no signs of dehydration
- Back pain — new onset, no neurological symptoms
- Ear infection, dental pain, eye irritation or minor conjunctivitis
- Mild asthma exacerbation — patient is using their inhaler effectively
- Minor sprain — able to bear weight
- Rash without fever or breathing involvement
- Mild fever in an adult (below 38.5°C) without other concerning symptoms

### System Action

1. Semi-Urgent flag raised in the patient management system.
2. Patient is registered and directed to the general waiting area.
3. Patient is informed of the approximate wait time.
4. Nurse performs observations (pulse, temperature) at intake.
5. Patient is reassessed if wait time exceeds 60 minutes or if the patient reports a change in symptoms.

### Who Is Notified

- Receiving department via the patient management system (no verbal notification required unless patient condition changes)
- Patient is given a call number and instructed to notify a nurse immediately if their symptoms worsen

---

## Level 4 — Non-Urgent

**Definition:** The patient's condition is stable, does not require same-day physician intervention, and can be managed with a scheduled appointment.

**Time to first assessment:** No urgent time constraint — patient may be offered a scheduled appointment.

### Examples of Non-Urgent Presentations

- Chronic condition follow-up that does not have new or worsening symptoms
- Routine medication refill request (patient should be directed to their GP or outpatient clinic)
- Results enquiry
- Mild, self-limiting conditions present for more than 48 hours with no worsening trend (e.g., mild cold without fever)
- Administrative requests (referral letters, medical reports)
- Minor wound check where there is no sign of infection

### System Action

1. Non-Urgent flag raised in the patient management system.
2. Patient is offered a scheduled appointment with the appropriate department (same day or within 48 hours).
3. Patient is informed that if their symptoms worsen before the appointment, they should return and report to triage.
4. If the patient is unhappy with a scheduled appointment and insists they need to be seen urgently, do not argue. Notify the senior triage nurse to reassess.

### Who Is Notified

- Scheduling team (to arrange appointment)
- Relevant department (via standard scheduling process)

## Escalation Rules — When to Upgrade an Urgency Level

Urgency levels must be escalated immediately if any of the following occur after initial classification:

1. The patient reports new symptoms that meet criteria for a higher level.
2. The patient's measured observations change: drop in blood pressure, fall in SpO2, rise in heart rate above 110 bpm in a resting adult.
3. The patient's appearance changes: they become pale, sweaty, confused, or are in obvious distress they were not in at intake.
4. The patient's wait time has exceeded the time target for their current level.
5. A family member or companion reports that the patient has become worse.

**Triage staff may escalate a level at any time on clinical instinct alone.** If something feels wrong, it is always correct to escalate and be proven overly cautious. It is never acceptable to downgrade a level or dismiss a concern without a qualified clinician review.

## Paediatric Patients — Adjusted Urgency Thresholds

Children under 12 years are more vulnerable to rapid deterioration and may not accurately communicate their symptoms. For paediatric patients, the following adjustments apply:

### Temperature Thresholds for Children

| Age | Fever Temperature for Urgent Routing | Emergency Route |
|---|---|---|
| Under 28 days (newborn) | Any fever above 38.0°C | Route to Emergency — no exceptions |
| 28 days to 3 months | Fever above 38.0°C | Route to Emergency |
| 3 months to 12 months | Fever above 38.5°C | Immediate or Urgent depending on behaviour |
| 12 months to 5 years | Fever above 39.5°C with any other symptom | Urgent minimum |
| 5 years to 12 years | Fever above 40°C | Urgent minimum |

### Behavioural Indicators (Children Under 12)

In children, behaviour is often a more reliable indicator of acuity than reported symptoms. Assign **Immediate** to any child who presents with:
- Unusual quietness or limpness
- Will not make eye contact or interact with parent or caregiver in a manner inconsistent with their normal behaviour (as reported by the caregiver)
- Inconsolable crying
- Difficulty breathing visible as rapid chest movement, nasal flaring, or ribcage visible on every breath (use of accessory muscles)

### Paediatric Notification Rule

For any paediatric patient assigned Urgent or above, the paediatric nurse on duty must be notified by verbal communication within 5 minutes of the level being assigned.

### Never Separate a Child from Their Caregiver

Regardless of urgency level, do not direct a child to any area without their accompanying parent or guardian. If no guardian is present, notify the senior triage nurse and the hospital's safeguarding officer immediately.
