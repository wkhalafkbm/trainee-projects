# Contract Compliance Review Checklist — First-Pass Review

**Document Purpose:** Systematic checklist for trainees and junior associates conducting first-pass review of commercial contracts. Complete every item before submitting your review memo to the supervising partner.
**Maintained By:** Commercial Practice Group, Al-Rashidi & Partners
**Version:** 2.4 (January 2023)

> **Instructions:** Work through each section in order. For each item, record: (1) whether the provision is present; (2) whether it meets the standard; (3) the specific clause reference in the reviewed contract; and (4) your flag note. Use the severity codes: **Must Fix** (deal-blocking — do not recommend signing), **Should Fix** (material concern — negotiate before signing), **Note Only** (document for client awareness but not blocking).

---

## Section 1: Parties and Authority

This section verifies that the parties are correctly and completely identified and that the signatories have authority to bind their respective entities.

---

### 1.1 Full Legal Name of Each Party

**What to look for:** The contract should state the full legal name of each party exactly as it appears in their commercial registration (CR). For Kuwaiti companies, this means the Arabic name and its transliteration. For foreign companies, the name as registered in their jurisdiction of incorporation.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- Trade names or brand names used instead of legal entity names (e.g., "Gulf Trading" instead of "Gulf Trading Company W.L.L.")
- No distinction between parent company and subsidiary — the wrong entity may be bound
- Inconsistent naming across the contract (entity referred to differently in different sections)

**Severity if missing or incorrect:** Must Fix
**Check:** [ ] Present and correct [ ] Needs clarification [ ] Missing

---

### 1.2 Commercial Registration Number

**What to look for:** For each Kuwaiti party, the contract should include the commercial registration (CR) number issued by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry. This confirms the entity is validly incorporated and trading legally.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- No CR number provided for a Kuwaiti entity
- CR number present but not verified against official records
- CR number present but company name does not match the registered name (potential fraud indicator — escalate immediately)

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix
**Check:** [ ] Present [ ] Not stated (request from client) [ ] Mismatch — escalate

---

### 1.3 Registered Address

**What to look for:** The registered business address of each party should be stated. This is the address for formal notices and service of process.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- Only a P.O. Box provided with no street address (insufficient for service of legal notices in many jurisdictions)
- Address does not match the entity's registered address in public records

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix
**Check:** [ ] Present [ ] P.O. Box only — flag [ ] Missing

---

### 1.4 Signatory Authority

**What to look for:** Confirm that the individual signing on behalf of each party has authority to do so. For Kuwaiti companies: check the Articles of Association or a current board resolution. For foreign companies: check the equivalent corporate document or a duly authenticated Power of Attorney.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- No representation in the contract that the signatory is authorised
- No supporting documentation provided (board resolution, POA) — request before execution
- Signatory is an employee with no apparent authority to bind the entity

**Severity if missing:** Must Fix — Do not recommend signing until authority is confirmed.
**Check:** [ ] Authority confirmed with documentation [ ] Documentation requested, pending [ ] Unknown — flag for partner

---

### 1.5 Foreign Party Compliance

**What to look for:** If a foreign company is a party, confirm whether it is required to have a registered local presence or a registered commercial agent in Kuwait (see Kuwait Commercial Law Reference, Section 8).

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- Foreign company contracting directly for services to be performed in Kuwait without a registered agent or local entity
- No reference to any local commercial registration or agency arrangement

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix — note regulatory risk for client
**Check:** [ ] Not applicable (no foreign party) [ ] Compliant [ ] Non-compliant — note risk

---

## Section 2: Commercial Terms

This section verifies that the core economic terms are clearly and completely defined.

---

### 2.1 Scope of Services / Deliverables

**What to look for:** The contract should clearly define what the Service Provider is obligated to deliver or perform. Vague descriptions create disputes about whether obligations have been met.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- Scope defined only in general terms (e.g., "IT services as agreed") without specifics
- Reference to an attached schedule or statement of work that is not actually attached
- No acceptance criteria for deliverables — the client cannot formally accept or reject work

**Severity if missing:** Must Fix (without a defined scope, the contract has no enforceable substance)
**Check:** [ ] Clearly defined [ ] Vague — negotiate [ ] Missing or referenced but not attached

---

### 2.2 Payment Terms

**What to look for:** The contract should state: (a) the total contract value or the basis for calculating fees; (b) the payment schedule (milestone-based, monthly, on delivery); (c) the invoice submission process; and (d) the payment period (days from invoice date).

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- Total value not stated or expressly left "to be agreed"
- Payment timing entirely at the Client's discretion
- No mechanism for raising or disputing invoices
- Payment period exceeds 60 days (flag — see Clause Library, Section 6)

**Severity if missing:** Must Fix
**Check:** [ ] Complete [ ] Incomplete — specify what is missing: ________________

---

### 2.3 Currency Specification

**What to look for:** All monetary amounts should state the currency. For Kuwait-law contracts, Kuwaiti Dinars (KD) is preferred. If USD or another currency is used, confirm whether there is a currency risk allocation provision.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- Amounts stated without a currency symbol (e.g., "500,000" — is this KD or USD? The difference is material)
- USD pricing with exchange rate determined unilaterally (see Clause Library, Section 6)
- Inconsistent currency references across the contract

**Severity if missing or inconsistent:** Must Fix
**Check:** [ ] Currency stated consistently [ ] Inconsistent — identify locations: ________________ [ ] Not stated

---

### 2.4 Delivery Obligations and Timeline

**What to look for:** Where the contract involves delivery of goods or completion of services by a date, confirm that: (a) the delivery date or timeline is specified; (b) the consequences of delay are addressed (penalty clause or damages); and (c) the client's obligations that are preconditions to delivery are identified.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- No delivery timeline — the Service Provider can perform at its own pace
- Penalty clause present but likely unenforceable as drafted (grossly excessive — see Kuwait Commercial Law Reference, Section 3)
- No recognition of the Client's responsibility to provide information, access, or approvals that enable performance

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix (depends on whether time is of the essence)
**Check:** [ ] Timeline defined [ ] Vague [ ] Missing

---

### 2.5 Variation and Change Order Process

**What to look for:** A mechanism for handling changes to the agreed scope — how changes are requested, agreed, and priced.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- No change order process — either party can claim scope has expanded without documented agreement on additional cost
- Service Provider can unilaterally change the scope or specifications

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix
**Check:** [ ] Present [ ] Missing

---

## Section 3: Risk Allocation

This section checks whether the contract adequately allocates risk between the parties.

---

### 3.1 Limitation of Liability

**What to look for:** A clause limiting each party's maximum financial exposure under the contract. Compare against the Clause Library standard (Section 1).

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- No limitation of liability clause — unlimited exposure for both parties
- Clause that is entirely one-sided (caps only one party's liability)
- Cap set at a nominal amount unrelated to contract value
- Standard carve-outs (death, personal injury, fraud) are absent

**Severity if missing:** Must Fix (significant financial exposure risk)
**Check:** [ ] Present and balanced [ ] Present but non-standard — flag details: ________________ [ ] Absent

---

### 3.2 Indemnification

**What to look for:** Which party indemnifies which, for what, and to what extent. Compare against the Clause Library standard (Section 2).

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- One-sided indemnity (Client indemnifies Service Provider for Service Provider's own negligence)
- Indemnity extends to first-party losses, not just third-party claims
- No contributory negligence carve-out

**Severity if non-standard:** Must Fix (can result in indeterminate liability exposure)
**Check:** [ ] Mutual and balanced [ ] One-sided — flag [ ] Absent (note for client)

---

### 3.3 Insurance Requirements

**What to look for:** Whether the contract requires either or both parties to maintain insurance cover (professional indemnity, public liability, employer's liability, etc.) and whether evidence of insurance must be provided.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- No insurance requirement at all — in the event of a major loss, the counterparty may be unable to satisfy a judgment
- Insurance required but no minimum coverage amounts stated
- No obligation to maintain insurance at renewal — coverage could lapse mid-contract

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix (for high-value or high-risk contracts), Note Only (for low-risk agreements)
**Check:** [ ] Present with adequate minimums [ ] Present but incomplete [ ] Absent

---

### 3.4 Warranties

**What to look for:** Express warranties given by each party (e.g., that goods conform to specification, that services will be performed with professional skill and care, that deliverables will not infringe third-party IP).

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- No warranties at all — the Client is left relying solely on statutory implied warranties
- Clause purporting to exclude all implied warranties and conditions to the maximum extent permitted by law — this may be enforceable in commercial contracts between sophisticated parties but should be highlighted

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix
**Check:** [ ] Present and adequate [ ] Present but exclude all implied terms — note for client [ ] Absent

---

## Section 4: Termination

This section checks whether the contract clearly defines how the relationship ends.

---

### 4.1 Termination for Cause

**What to look for:** The circumstances in which either party may terminate immediately or on notice for the other's material breach. Common triggers: insolvency, material breach unremedied after a cure period, persistent minor breaches.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- No termination for cause provision — a party in breach cannot be removed from the contract without litigation
- Cure period is unreasonably short (less than 14 days for complex breaches) or absent entirely
- Only one party has termination for cause rights

**Severity if missing:** Must Fix
**Check:** [ ] Both parties have rights [ ] One-sided — note which party is disadvantaged [ ] Absent

---

### 4.2 Termination for Convenience

**What to look for:** The right of either or both parties to exit the contract without cause, with adequate notice. Compare against the Clause Library standard (Section 3).

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- Only the Client has a termination for convenience right (note — this is commercially common but should be flagged if the Service Provider has no reciprocal right)
- Termination right with no notice period or an unreasonably short notice period
- No payment for work already performed upon termination for convenience

**Severity if missing or one-sided:** Should Fix
**Check:** [ ] Mutual with reasonable notice [ ] One-sided — note details [ ] Absent

---

### 4.3 Notice Periods

**What to look for:** All termination provisions should specify notice periods (how many days, to whom, in what form). Notice periods should be commercially reasonable.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- Termination "immediately" with no notice, for any breach (even minor and curable)
- No definition of how notice is to be given (written? email? registered post?) — creates disputes about whether valid notice was given

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix
**Check:** [ ] Defined and reasonable [ ] Undefined or unreasonable — flag details: ________________

---

### 4.4 Consequences of Termination

**What to look for:** What happens upon termination — payment of amounts accrued; return of confidential information or materials; survival of ongoing obligations (confidentiality, IP, limitation of liability); wind-down obligations.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- Contract is silent on consequences — the parties would have to negotiate or litigate on termination
- No survival clause — obligations that should outlast the contract (confidentiality, indemnities) expire on termination

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix
**Check:** [ ] Addressed [ ] Partially addressed — note gaps: ________________ [ ] Silent

---

## Section 5: Governing Law and Jurisdiction

---

### 5.1 Governing Law

**What to look for:** The contract should expressly state which country's law governs the interpretation and enforcement of the contract. For contracts with a Kuwait nexus, Kuwait law is strongly preferred.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- No governing law clause — Kuwait courts will apply Kuwait law by default, but this creates uncertainty
- Foreign governing law that conflicts with mandatory Kuwait legal protections
- Governing law is inconsistent with the jurisdiction clause (e.g., English law + Kuwait courts — creates complexity)

**Severity if missing:** Must Fix
**Check:** [ ] Kuwait law specified [ ] Foreign law — flag and note mandatory Kuwait law considerations [ ] Absent

---

### 5.2 Jurisdiction Clause

**What to look for:** The clause designating which courts have authority to hear disputes (or whether disputes are to be arbitrated — see Section 5.3). Confirm: (a) whether jurisdiction is exclusive or non-exclusive; and (b) whether it is enforceable under Kuwait law for the transaction type.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- Exclusive foreign jurisdiction for obligations to be performed in Kuwait — may be unenforceable
- Jurisdiction in a country where enforcement of a judgment would be practically impossible
- No jurisdiction clause at all — parties may not know where to sue

**Severity if missing or problematic:** Must Fix
**Check:** [ ] Kuwait jurisdiction specified [ ] Foreign jurisdiction — assess enforceability [ ] Absent

---

### 5.3 Arbitration Clause

**What to look for:** If the parties have chosen arbitration instead of litigation, confirm the arbitration clause satisfies the requirements under Kuwait Law No. 11/1995 (see Kuwait Commercial Law Reference, Section 5). Check: institution, seat, rules, language, number of arbitrators.

**What a missing or problematic provision looks like:**
- Ad hoc arbitration with no institutional framework
- Service Provider-appointed arbitrator (see Clause Library, Section 7)
- No provision for urgent interim relief
- Government party without evidence of Council of Ministers approval

**Severity if non-standard:** Must Fix (non-standard arbitration clause may be unenforceable)
**Check:** [ ] Not applicable (jurisdiction clause used instead) [ ] Compliant with Law No. 11/1995 [ ] Non-compliant — flag details: ________________

---

## Section 6: Missing Provisions

This section identifies clauses that are commonly expected in commercial contracts but are entirely absent from the reviewed document.

---

### 6.1 Entire Agreement Clause

**What to look for:** A clause confirming the written contract supersedes all prior negotiations, representations, and agreements. Protects against claims based on pre-contractual representations.

**Severity if missing:** Note Only (unless there is a known prior negotiation where misrepresentations were made — in that case, Should Fix)
**Check:** [ ] Present [ ] Absent — note

---

### 6.2 Notices Clause

**What to look for:** A clause specifying how formal notices must be given (method, address, deemed receipt timing). Without this, disputes arise about whether notices were validly given.

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix
**Check:** [ ] Present [ ] Absent

---

### 6.3 Waiver Clause

**What to look for:** A clause confirming that failure to exercise a right does not constitute a waiver of that right on future occasions.

**Severity if missing:** Note Only
**Check:** [ ] Present [ ] Absent

---

### 6.4 Severability Clause

**What to look for:** A clause confirming that if any provision is found to be unenforceable, the remainder of the contract remains in force.

**Severity if missing:** Note Only (Kuwait courts will generally read down or sever unenforceable provisions, but an express clause is preferable)
**Check:** [ ] Present [ ] Absent

---

### 6.5 Amendment Clause

**What to look for:** A clause confirming that amendments to the contract must be in writing and signed by authorised representatives of both parties. Prevents informal oral modifications.

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix
**Check:** [ ] Present [ ] Absent

---

### 6.6 Anti-Bribery / Compliance Clause

**What to look for:** For contracts above a certain value threshold or involving government entities, the firm's standard practice is to include a representation that neither party will engage in corrupt practices in connection with the contract.

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix (for government contracts or contracts above KD 50,000), Note Only (other contracts)
**Check:** [ ] Present [ ] Absent — note value/nature of contract

---

### 6.7 Data Protection / Privacy Clause

**What to look for:** If the contract involves processing, transferring, or storing personal data, a data protection clause should be present addressing compliance with Kuwait's applicable privacy requirements.

**Severity if missing:** Should Fix (if personal data is involved), Note Only (if no personal data)
**Check:** [ ] Not applicable [ ] Present [ ] Absent — flag if personal data is involved
