# Standard Clause Library — Contract Review Reference

**Document Purpose:** Library of standard and non-standard contract clauses for use during first-pass review. For each clause type, trainees should compare the submitted contract language against the firm's standard position and flag deviations.
**Maintained By:** Commercial Practice Group, Al-Rashidi & Partners
**Version:** 3.1 (January 2023)

> **How to use this library:** When reviewing a contract, locate the relevant clause type below. Compare the contract language to the Standard Version. If the contract language resembles the Non-Standard Version (or is worse), apply the indicated Flag Severity and Suggested Action. If the clause is absent entirely, flag as "Missing Clause" with severity equivalent to the non-standard version or higher.

---

## 1. Limitation of Liability Clause

### Standard Version (Firm-Approved)

> "Neither party's liability to the other under or in connection with this Agreement shall exceed the total fees paid or payable by the Client to the Service Provider in the twelve (12) months immediately preceding the event giving rise to the claim. This limitation applies to all claims whether in contract, tort (including negligence), breach of statutory duty, or otherwise. The foregoing limitation does not apply to: (a) death or personal injury caused by negligence; (b) fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation; or (c) any liability that cannot be limited or excluded by applicable law."

**Why this is standard:** The 12-month cap is proportionate to the contract value. The carve-outs preserve exposure for the most serious wrongs while providing commercial certainty.

---

### Non-Standard Version (Flag This)

> "The Service Provider's liability under this Agreement shall be limited to KD 500 (five hundred Kuwaiti Dinars) regardless of the nature or magnitude of the claim."

**Flag Severity:** HIGH

**Why this is problematic:**
- The fixed cap (KD 500) bears no relationship to the contract value. On a multi-million dinar engagement, this is effectively a liability waiver in favour of the Service Provider.
- The clause omits the standard carve-outs for death, personal injury, and fraud — these carve-outs are required as a matter of public policy and their absence does not mean the service provider escapes liability for them, but the clause creates confusion.
- The clause is one-sided: it caps only the Service Provider's liability, leaving the Client exposed to uncapped liability. This asymmetry should be highlighted for the client.

**Suggested Action:** REJECT / Negotiate to mutual cap tied to contract value with standard carve-outs.

## 2. Indemnification Clause

### Standard Version (Firm-Approved)

> "Each party (the 'Indemnifying Party') shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless the other party and its officers, directors, employees, and agents (the 'Indemnified Party') from and against any third-party claims, losses, damages, costs, and expenses (including reasonable legal fees) arising out of or resulting from the Indemnifying Party's: (a) breach of this Agreement; (b) negligence or wilful misconduct; or (c) infringement of any third-party intellectual property rights. Neither party's indemnification obligation shall extend to claims caused or contributed to by the Indemnified Party's own negligence or wilful misconduct."

**Why this is standard:** Mutual structure, limited to third-party claims, with a contributory negligence carve-out. This reflects a balanced allocation of risk.

---

### Non-Standard Version (Flag This)

> "The Client shall indemnify the Service Provider against all and any losses, claims, damages, costs, penalties, fines, and expenses of whatever nature arising in any way connected with the performance of the Services or the Client's use of any deliverable, whether or not caused by the Service Provider's negligence."

**Flag Severity:** HIGH

**Why this is problematic:**
- The indemnity runs in one direction only: the Client indemnifies the Service Provider, but the Service Provider owes no corresponding indemnity.
- The phrase "arising in any way connected with" is extremely broad and could capture losses caused entirely by the Service Provider's own failure.
- The explicit inclusion of losses caused by "the Service Provider's negligence" is particularly aggressive — the Client is being asked to absorb the consequences of the other side's own wrongdoing.
- Under Kuwait law (Article 207, Civil Code), parties may agree to indemnify for losses caused by their own negligence, but such a clause would be scrutinised closely by a Kuwait court and may be unenforceable if unconscionable.

**Suggested Action:** REJECT. Request a mutual indemnity limited to each party's own breach, negligence, and IP infringement, with a contributory negligence carve-out.

## 3. Termination for Convenience Clause

### Standard Version (Firm-Approved)

> "Either party may terminate this Agreement for convenience upon not less than sixty (60) days' prior written notice to the other party. In the event of termination for convenience by the Client, the Client shall pay the Service Provider for all work properly performed up to the date of termination, plus reasonable demobilisation costs documented and submitted within thirty (30) days of the termination notice. No other compensation shall be payable by either party in respect of a termination for convenience."

**Why this is standard:** Mutual termination right with a reasonable notice period. Demobilisation costs are acknowledged but capped by a documentation and timing requirement.

---

### Non-Standard Version (Flag This)

> "The Client may terminate this Agreement at any time without notice and without liability."

**Flag Severity:** MEDIUM (HIGH if client is the Service Provider)

**Why this is problematic:**
- "Without notice" exposes the Service Provider to abrupt termination with no opportunity to wind down operations or reassign resources, causing avoidable loss.
- "Without liability" attempts to extinguish any claim for work already performed or costs already incurred. Under Kuwait law, a party cannot generally enrich itself at another's expense — a court may imply reasonable compensation even if the clause purports to exclude it (unjust enrichment principles under Article 232, Civil Code).
- The clause is unilateral: only the Client has a termination right. The Service Provider has no reciprocal right to exit a failing or non-paying relationship.
- Absence of any demobilisation or wind-down provision can cause significant operational and financial harm.

**Suggested Action:** NEGOTIATE. Minimum 30-day notice period; payment for work performed to date; mutual termination right.

## 4. Intellectual Property Ownership Clause

### Standard Version (Firm-Approved)

> "All intellectual property rights in deliverables created specifically for the Client under this Agreement ('Bespoke Deliverables') shall vest in the Client upon full payment of the applicable fees. The Service Provider retains all intellectual property rights in its pre-existing materials, tools, methodologies, and know-how ('Background IP'), and grants the Client a non-exclusive, non-transferable licence to use the Background IP solely to the extent necessary to use the Bespoke Deliverables for the purposes contemplated by this Agreement."

**Why this is standard:** Clean separation between bespoke deliverables (client owns) and background IP (service provider retains with a use licence). Ownership is contingent on payment, which protects the service provider.

---

### Non-Standard Version (Flag This)

> "All work product, deliverables, concepts, ideas, inventions, and improvements developed by the Service Provider in the course of performing the Services, whether or not included in a deliverable, shall remain the sole and exclusive property of the Service Provider. The Client receives no rights in any such materials except a limited right to use deliverables for internal purposes only."

**Flag Severity:** HIGH

**Why this is problematic:**
- The Client is paying for deliverables but receives only a limited internal-use licence — the Service Provider retains ownership of everything including bespoke materials created at the Client's expense.
- "Concepts, ideas, and improvements" sweeps in materials beyond discrete deliverables, potentially including innovations or methodologies the Client contributed to or directed.
- "Internal purposes only" restricts the Client from using deliverables commercially, which may entirely frustrate the purpose of the engagement.
- If the service is software development, website design, or creation of any commercial asset, this clause effectively means the Client does not own the asset it paid to create.

**Suggested Action:** REJECT. Ownership of bespoke deliverables must vest in the Client. Background IP retention by the Service Provider is acceptable with an appropriate licence.

## 5. Confidentiality / NDA Clause

### Standard Version (Firm-Approved)

> "Each party ('Receiving Party') agrees to hold in strict confidence all Confidential Information received from the other party ('Disclosing Party') and to use such Confidential Information solely for the purpose of performing its obligations under this Agreement. The Receiving Party shall not disclose Confidential Information to any third party without the prior written consent of the Disclosing Party, except to its employees, officers, and professional advisers on a need-to-know basis and subject to equivalent confidentiality obligations. 'Confidential Information' means all non-public information disclosed by the Disclosing Party in connection with this Agreement, except information that: (a) is or becomes publicly available through no breach of this Agreement; (b) was already known to the Receiving Party prior to disclosure; (c) is independently developed by the Receiving Party without use of Confidential Information; or (d) is required to be disclosed by applicable law or court order, provided the Receiving Party gives prompt prior notice where legally permitted. These obligations survive termination for a period of five (5) years."

**Why this is standard:** Mutual, with standard carve-outs, a 5-year post-termination period, and a permitted disclosure mechanism for legal compulsion.

---

### Non-Standard Version (Flag This)

> "The Client agrees that all information provided by the Service Provider is confidential and shall not be disclosed for a period of ten (10) years. The Service Provider shall have no obligation of confidentiality in respect of any information provided by the Client."

**Flag Severity:** HIGH

**Why this is problematic:**
- Entirely one-sided: the Client bears confidentiality obligations; the Service Provider bears none. The Service Provider can freely share the Client's proprietary information, trade secrets, or commercially sensitive data.
- A 10-year obligation on the Client is unusually long and commercially burdensome, particularly for information that may lose sensitivity well before that period.
- No carve-outs for information that enters the public domain or was already known — the Client is bound even if the information becomes publicly available.
- In many commercial relationships (professional services, technology, finance), the Client shares far more sensitive information than the Service Provider. This clause inverts the typical risk allocation.

**Suggested Action:** REJECT. Confidentiality must be mutual. Post-termination period should be 3–5 years. Standard carve-outs must be included.

## 6. Payment Terms Clause

### Standard Version (Firm-Approved)

> "The Client shall pay each invoice submitted by the Service Provider within thirty (30) calendar days of the invoice date. All amounts are stated in Kuwaiti Dinars (KD) and are exclusive of applicable taxes. Invoices not disputed in good faith within fourteen (14) days of receipt are deemed accepted. Late payments shall bear interest at the rate of 2% per annum above the Central Bank of Kuwait overnight lending rate, accruing daily from the due date until the date of actual payment. The Service Provider may suspend performance upon thirty (30) days' written notice if any undisputed invoice remains unpaid beyond forty-five (45) days of its due date."

**Why this is standard:** 30-day payment cycle, clear currency, a dispute mechanism that prevents bad-faith disputes of entire invoices, a reasonable late-payment interest rate, and a suspension right as a proportionate remedy.

---

### Non-Standard Version (Flag This)

> "The Client shall pay invoices within ninety (90) days of receipt. The Service Provider shall have no right to charge interest on late payments. Payment shall be made in USD at the exchange rate determined by the Client at the time of payment."

**Flag Severity:** MEDIUM

**Why this is problematic:**
- A 90-day payment cycle is unusually long and significantly disadvantages the Service Provider's cash flow — well beyond market standard.
- Excluding interest on late payment removes the only financial incentive for timely payment and leaves the Service Provider with no remedy short of legal action for delayed payments.
- Permitting the Client to determine the exchange rate unilaterally creates material currency risk for the Service Provider on USD-denominated invoices where KD is the local currency — the Client has an incentive to select an unfavourable rate.
- No suspension or termination right for persistent non-payment.

**Suggested Action:** NEGOTIATE. Payment within 45 days maximum; interest at a defined rate; exchange rate fixed at a published reference rate (e.g., Central Bank of Kuwait mid-market rate on the invoice date).

## 7. Dispute Resolution Clause

### Standard Version (Firm-Approved)

> "The parties shall attempt to resolve any dispute arising out of or in connection with this Agreement through good-faith negotiation for a period of thirty (30) days following written notice by either party identifying the dispute ('Negotiation Period'). If the dispute is not resolved within the Negotiation Period, either party may refer the dispute to binding arbitration administered by the Kuwait Chamber of Commerce and Industry Arbitration Centre in accordance with its rules in force at the time of the referral. The seat of arbitration shall be Kuwait City. The arbitration shall be conducted in Arabic and English. The arbitral tribunal shall consist of one (1) arbitrator agreed upon by the parties, or failing agreement within fourteen (14) days, appointed by the Kuwait Chamber of Commerce and Industry. The arbitral award shall be final and binding. Nothing in this clause prevents either party from seeking urgent interim relief from a competent court."

**Why this is standard:** Escalating mechanism (negotiation first), binding arbitration with a defined institutional framework, and a carve-out for urgent interim relief.

---

### Non-Standard Version (Flag This)

> "Any dispute shall be finally determined by a sole arbitrator appointed by the Service Provider at its sole discretion. The arbitration shall be conducted under rules determined by the Service Provider. The Client waives any right to challenge the arbitral award before any court."

**Flag Severity:** HIGH

**Why this is problematic:**
- The Service Provider appointing its own arbitrator is a fundamental breach of the principle of arbitral neutrality. No reputable arbitral institution permits this, and Kuwait courts may refuse to enforce an award issued under such a process on grounds of procedural unfairness.
- Rules "determined by the Service Provider" grants the Service Provider unilateral control over the procedural conduct of any dispute — it could change rules mid-arbitration to its advantage.
- Waiver of any right to challenge the award is of questionable enforceability under Kuwait law (Article 11, Law No. 11/1995 permits challenge on grounds of procedural irregularity and public policy violations); courts may void such a waiver.
- The absence of an institutional framework or defined rules makes this clause legally uncertain and practically unworkable.

**Suggested Action:** REJECT. Replace with a neutral institutional arbitration clause (Kuwait Chamber of Commerce, ICC, or LCIA) with standard appointment procedures and award challenge rights preserved.

## Clause Severity Summary

| Clause Type | Non-Standard Version Issue | Flag Severity | Suggested Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Limitation of Liability | Fixed low cap; no carve-outs; one-sided | HIGH | Reject / Negotiate |
| Indemnification | One-sided; covers Service Provider's own negligence | HIGH | Reject |
| Termination for Convenience | No notice; no payment for work done; one-sided | MEDIUM–HIGH | Negotiate |
| IP Ownership | Service Provider retains all IP in bespoke work | HIGH | Reject |
| Confidentiality | One-sided; no standard carve-outs | HIGH | Reject |
| Payment Terms | 90-day terms; no interest; unilateral exchange rate | MEDIUM | Negotiate |
| Dispute Resolution | Self-appointing arbitrator; waiver of challenge rights | HIGH | Reject |
