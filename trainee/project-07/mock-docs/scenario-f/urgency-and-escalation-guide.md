# Urgency Classification and Escalation Guide

**Document:** Kuwait Municipality — Public Complaints Management System
**Applies To:** Complaint Intake Officers, Supervisors, Department Heads
**Version:** 3.0
**Effective Date:** March 2023
**Issuing Department:** Municipal Affairs and Customer Service Directorate

---

## Purpose

This guide defines how complaints submitted to Kuwait Municipality are classified by urgency level. Correct classification ensures that public safety issues receive immediate attention while routine maintenance matters are handled efficiently within standard timelines. All intake officers must apply this guide at the point of complaint registration. If there is uncertainty about the appropriate urgency level, always default to the higher classification and consult a supervisor.

---

## Urgency Level 1 — Urgent

**Response Requirement:** Initial attendance or escalation within **4 hours** of complaint registration.
**Acknowledgement to Complainant:** Immediate automated acknowledgement upon registration, followed by a personal follow-up call within 1 hour confirming that a response team has been dispatched.

### Definition

An Urgent complaint involves an active, unmitigated risk to the safety, health, or property of members of the public. The situation requires immediate intervention and cannot be deferred to normal working hours.

Urgent classification applies 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including weekends and public holidays.

### Examples of Urgent Complaints

**Road and Traffic Safety:**
- An open pothole, collapsed road surface, or road damage large enough to cause a vehicle accident, particularly on a main road or at a road junction
- A fallen sign, damaged traffic signal, or missing road barrier in a live traffic area
- Road flooding that is causing vehicles to stall or diverting traffic onto unsafe routes
- A road or pavement collapse linked to an underground cavity or drainage failure

**Electrical and Lighting Hazards:**
- An exposed live electrical cable or damaged street lighting pole with visible electrical hazard
- A fallen power distribution box or exposed wiring accessible to pedestrians
- Street lighting failure across an entire main road or at a dangerous pedestrian crossing

**Structural Risk:**
- A building or structure showing signs of imminent collapse onto a public area (visible cracking, leaning, or partial collapse already in progress)
- A large tree that has partially fallen and is at risk of completing its fall onto a road or occupied public area

**Flooding and Drainage:**
- Flooding that is directly affecting residential properties or commercial premises (water entering buildings)
- Road flooding that has created a water depth hazard for vehicles or pedestrians
- A burst municipal water main causing road subsidence or flooding of a public road

**Public Health — Immediate Risk:**
- A confirmed or strongly suspected report of contaminated food currently being served or sold at a commercial premises
- A large vermin infestation (rats, insects) at or immediately adjacent to a school, hospital, food market, or mosque
- A hazardous waste spill or the discovery of hazardous substances on public land

### Who Is Notified for Urgent Complaints

1. The relevant department's on-call duty officer (all departments maintain a 24/7 duty rota)
2. The Complaints Management Supervisor on duty
3. The relevant department head is notified by SMS if the complaint is not acknowledged by the duty officer within 30 minutes of registration
4. If the complaint involves a public safety risk affecting a significant number of people (e.g., major road flooding, structural collapse), the Director General's office is notified

### Acknowledgement to Complainant

Upon registration of an Urgent complaint:
1. An automated SMS and (if email provided) email acknowledgement is sent immediately, including the complaint reference number and the 4-hour response commitment.
2. A follow-up call is made by the intake officer or supervisor within 1 hour to confirm that a response team has been mobilised.
3. If the complainant cannot be reached by phone, an SMS is sent confirming mobilisation.

## Urgency Level 2 — Standard

**Response Requirement:** Initial acknowledgement within **1 business day**; inspection or substantive response within **2 business days**; resolution or scheduled repair within **5 business days** (may vary by category — refer to Service Categories and Routing Guide).
**Acknowledgement to Complainant:** Automated acknowledgement immediately upon registration. Personal follow-up by the handling department within 2 business days.

### Definition

A Standard complaint relates to a maintenance, quality, or service delivery issue that is genuine and requires attention but does not present an immediate risk to public safety or health. The issue may be causing inconvenience, environmental degradation, or deterioration that will worsen if not addressed, but it can be managed within normal working hours.

### Examples of Standard Complaints

**Infrastructure and Maintenance:**
- Potholes that are causing inconvenience but are not large enough to constitute an immediate accident hazard
- A non-functioning street light on a secondary road or residential street
- Damaged benches or park furniture
- A broken drain grating that does not represent an immediate fall hazard (pedestrian area is not heavily used)
- Litter accumulation in a public park or on a public road

**Waste and Cleanliness:**
- Missed waste collection from a residential area
- Overflowing public bins (where the overflow is not creating an immediate health hazard)
- Accumulated waste on a vacant plot that has been building up over several days

**Noise and Compliance:**
- Construction noise during permitted hours but at levels that appear to exceed permitted limits
- Commercial premises operating in a manner inconsistent with their planning permit

**Building and Planning:**
- Suspected permit violation for construction work that is not currently active or expanding
- A minor encroachment onto a pavement or public road

**Public Health — Non-Urgent:**
- A pest control request for a commercial premises where there is no active food handling risk
- General cleanliness concerns at a commercial premises

### Who Is Notified for Standard Complaints

1. The relevant department's complaints inbox (automated routing via the case management system)
2. The assigned case officer is notified by the system and is responsible for acknowledging and scheduling a response within the SLA
3. The Complaints Management Supervisor receives a daily dashboard showing all open Standard complaints approaching their SLA deadline

### Acknowledgement to Complainant

Upon registration of a Standard complaint:
1. An automated SMS and (if email provided) email acknowledgement is sent immediately, including the complaint reference number and the applicable SLA date.
2. The acknowledgement informs the complainant that a case officer will be in contact within 2 business days with an update.
3. If no update is provided within 2 business days, the system sends a reminder to the case officer and notifies the department supervisor.

## Urgency Level 3 — Scheduled

**Response Requirement:** Acknowledgement within **1 business day**; assessment or first substantive response within **5 business days**; resolution timeline determined by the nature of the request (may involve inclusion in the annual maintenance or capital works plan).
**Acknowledgement to Complainant:** Automated acknowledgement immediately upon registration. The complainant is informed that the request has been noted for planned scheduling.

### Definition

A Scheduled complaint or request covers matters that are low-priority, cosmetic, or enhancement-oriented — issues that do not present any safety risk and are not causing material inconvenience to the public. These are typically improvement requests, forward-planning requests, or complaints about conditions that have been stable for an extended period.

### Examples of Scheduled Complaints

**Cosmetic and Aesthetic Issues:**
- Faded road markings or worn-but-functional pavement surfaces
- Aging but structurally sound park furniture
- General appearance concerns (e.g., an area looks untidy but is clean and safe)
- Overgrown vegetation in a public area that is not causing a safety or visibility hazard

**Service Requests and New Provision:**
- Request for a new public bin in an area currently unserved
- Request for new street lighting on a road that currently has no lighting but is not a high-traffic area
- Request to increase frequency of waste collection in an area
- Request for new playground equipment in an existing park

**Low-Priority Improvements:**
- A road marking that needs refreshing but remains legible
- Suggestions for improvement to a public space layout
- Historical complaints about conditions that have since improved (submitted after the fact for record purposes)

### Who Is Notified for Scheduled Complaints

1. The relevant department's planning inbox (automated routing)
2. The department's planning coordinator reviews Scheduled complaints when preparing quarterly and annual work plans
3. No individual case officer is assigned for routine follow-up — complainants are advised that Scheduled matters are addressed as resources allow

### Acknowledgement to Complainant

Upon registration of a Scheduled complaint:
1. An automated SMS and (if email provided) email acknowledgement is sent immediately, including the complaint reference number.
2. The acknowledgement informs the complainant that their request has been registered and will be assessed for inclusion in scheduled maintenance or improvement works. The complainant is advised that a definitive timeline cannot be provided at registration.
3. If the request is assessed and included in a works plan, the complainant is informed by SMS of the approximate scheduled timeframe.

## Section 4: Complaints Requiring Mandatory Management Escalation

Regardless of the urgency level classification, certain complaints must be escalated immediately to senior management. These complaints require oversight beyond normal case management due to their nature, public profile, or implications for the municipality's reputation and operations.

---

### 4.1 Complaints from Media Representatives or Public Figures

**Trigger:** A complaint is identified as being submitted by, or on behalf of:
- A journalist or media organisation
- A member of the National Assembly (MP)
- A senior government official (Director General or above in any ministry)
- A foreign diplomatic mission or embassy
- A public figure with a significant social media following (verified account, substantial reach)

**Action Required:**
1. Do not treat the complaint as a routine case. Register it in the system with the "VIP / Media Flag" designation.
2. Notify the Director of Municipal Affairs and Customer Service Directorate within 1 hour of registration, regardless of the time of day.
3. The Director (or their designated deputy) will determine the response approach and may handle communication directly.
4. Standard SLAs are overridden — a personalised written response must be issued within 24 hours regardless of the urgency level.
5. Do not make any public statements about the complaint without prior approval from the Communications Department.

---

### 4.2 Repeat Complaints Without Resolution (Three-Strike Escalation)

**Trigger:** A complaint is identified as the third or subsequent submission by the same complainant about the same unresolved issue — meaning the complainant has received a resolution-acknowledged response on at least two prior occasions but the issue has recurred or was not, in fact, resolved.

**How to Identify:**
- The intake system automatically flags cases where the same complainant (matched by Civil ID or phone number) has submitted a complaint about the same location and category more than twice within a 90-day rolling period.
- Officers must also manually check during intake if the complainant mentions a prior complaint reference number.

**Action Required:**
1. Register the complaint and apply the "Repeat Complaint — Escalation Required" flag in the system.
2. Notify the relevant department head immediately (same business day). The department head is personally responsible for reviewing the history of the prior complaints and responding.
3. A root cause assessment is mandatory: the department must explain in writing why the issue was not resolved on prior occasions.
4. The complainant must receive a personal (non-automated) call from the department supervisor within 1 business day.
5. All three-strike cases are included in the monthly management report reviewed by the Director General.

---

### 4.3 Complaints Involving Alleged Misconduct by Municipality Staff

**Trigger:** A complaint in which the complainant alleges that a municipality employee has:
- Behaved improperly, rudely, or abusively toward the complainant or a member of the public
- Accepted or solicited a bribe or unofficial payment in connection with a municipality service
- Deliberately delayed or obstructed the processing of a complaint or service request
- Carried out an inspection or issued a permit in a manner that suggests corruption or improper conduct

**Action Required:**
1. Register the complaint in the system with the "Staff Conduct — Confidential" designation. This complaint type is visible only to authorised management users — do not discuss its contents with other field staff.
2. Notify the Head of Internal Audit and Compliance within 2 hours of registration.
3. Notify the Director of Municipal Affairs and Customer Service Directorate by the end of the same business day.
4. The complaint is removed from normal SLA tracking and transferred to the Internal Audit process. The complainant is informed that their complaint is being reviewed at a senior level and will receive a response within 5 business days.
5. Under no circumstances should the alleged staff member be informally briefed about the complaint prior to the formal Internal Audit process. This could constitute interference with an investigation.
6. If the complaint involves alleged bribery, the matter must be reported to the Anti-Corruption Authority (Nazaha) in accordance with Law No. 2/2016.

## Urgency Classification Quick Reference

| Situation | Level | Initial Response |
|---|---|---|
| Road hazard causing or likely to cause an accident | Urgent | 4 hours |
| Exposed electrical hazard accessible to the public | Urgent | 4 hours |
| Flooding affecting properties or blocking roads | Urgent | 4 hours |
| Structural collapse risk to a public area | Urgent | 4 hours |
| Contaminated food currently on sale | Urgent | 4 hours |
| Non-functioning street light on residential road | Standard | 2 business days |
| Missed waste collection | Standard | 1–2 business days |
| Suspected building permit violation (inactive site) | Standard | 3 business days |
| General park maintenance | Standard | 5 business days |
| Request for new public bin | Scheduled | 5 business days |
| Faded road markings (legible but worn) | Scheduled | Planned cycle |
| Cosmetic improvement request | Scheduled | Planned cycle |
| Complaint from MP or media | Escalate immediately | 24 hours — management response |
| Third repeat complaint (same issue) | Escalate to dept. head | Same day |
| Alleged staff misconduct or bribery | Escalate to Internal Audit | 2 hours |
